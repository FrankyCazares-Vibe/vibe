## 1. The short version

- **Best first move for users (not money): the Jaguars.** Ask athletics, and IU Licensing and Trademarks, for a written OK to show the game calendar in the "What's the vibe tonight?" card, and pitch a Club & Greek Night. It costs nothing and earns nothing. The first evening men's basketball home games (Sat Nov 7, 5 p.m.; Wed Nov 11, 6:30 p.m.) are too soon: we'd need two written OKs, a build, and enough users to clear the 5-person floor on a "going" count. Aim at a January home game or Winter Homecoming (Sun Feb 7, 2 p.m.) [[71]](https://iuindyjags.com/calendar.ashx/calendar.ics?sport_id=0). The new arena holds about 4,200-4,500 (sources differ) [[69]](https://cpf.iu.edu/capital-projects/projects/athletics/james-t-morris-arena.html) [[70]](https://www.wrtv.com/sports/college/take-a-look-at-iu-indys-new-arena-set-to-open-this-fall). Last season's 1,059 average came mostly in "The Jungle" [[73]](https://iuindyjags.com/sports/2025/10/24/iu-indy-mens-basketball-2025-26-promotional-schedule.aspx), so it says little about demand.
- **Second: SAE, then other IFC chapters one by one.** Franky's IFC seat is a conflict to manage, not a shortcut: SAE is Franky's and James's own chapter, and one of only 5 IFC chapters [[15]](https://life.indianapolis.iu.edu/get-involved/fraternities-sororities/index.html). Tell FSL first, have Franky sit out any IFC discussion or vote about Vibe, and let each chapter opt in on its own. Start with event tools that make no safety claims: registration, reminders, a guest list. This is the route most likely to get us real clubs.
- **Possible money, much later: national fraternity and sorority headquarters (HQs).** Members already pay them per head for risk and insurance, but we found no HQ or insurer paying for outside software, and each national has at most one chapter here. An HQ will want its own chapters on several campuses using the tools first. Our guess: 2028 at the earliest.
- **Then: local businesses.** Make the list of 20 now. Offer free spots only once we have real daily users and the ad terms and "Sponsored" label are live, and charge only after that. Student housing waits until reach is proven.
- **The school is a partner, not a buyer, for now.** Ask for permission and visibility. Get Procurement Services' OK before we flyer, chalk or table on campus: IU's solicitation ban covers students too [[47]](https://policies.iu.edu/policies/fin-purch-12-non-solicitation-on-campus/index.html).
- **Skip for now:** selling member data, charging clubs, political (PAC) money, paying athletes, alcohol ads. Housing corporations wait until FSL tells us whether any chapter has a house.
- **Every paid route needs:** users, the LLC bank account, EIN and a business address (for Stripe and vendor forms), business insurance (schools, HQs and athletics ask for proof), and lawyer-reviewed terms. New data, like guest lists, means a same-day Privacy Policy change.

## 2. Area by area

### 2a. National fraternities and sororities, and prevention money

**What we found**

- The federal Stop Campus Hazing Act (Dec 23, 2024) makes schools count hazing in any student group, not just Greek life, and run prevention programs, with no federal money [[1]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-118publ173/html/PLAW-118publ173.htm). Schools had to post a hazing transparency report by Dec 23, 2025, and the first hazing counts (for 2025) were due in the Oct 1, 2026 security reports [[2]](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/stop-campus-hazing-act-first-federal-anti-hazing-law-creates-new-reporting-requirements-for-higher-ed-institutions). We have not read IU Indianapolis's 2026 report yet.
- IU provides a Vector Solutions hazing prevention module to all students [[3]](https://www.protect.iu.edu/iu-police-department/campus-security-reports/annual-security-reports/2025/11-hazing/index.html), delivered here through One.IU [[4]](https://life.indianapolis.iu.edu/community-standards/hazing/index.html). Indiana law (IC 21-39-9.1) also requires hazing training for student organization leaders, per IU's report [[3]](https://www.protect.iu.edu/iu-police-department/campus-security-reports/annual-security-reports/2025/11-hazing/index.html). Don't sell IU a course.
- Purdue Indianapolis lists no hazing findings for 2024-25 or 2025-26 [[5]](https://purdue.edu/odos/osrr/resources/documents/disciplinary-status-indianapolis.php). IU keeps its own list of hazing findings by student organizations [[3]](https://www.protect.iu.edu/iu-police-department/campus-security-reports/annual-security-reports/2025/11-hazing/index.html); we haven't checked it for IU Indianapolis. Pitch event logistics and records the chapter controls, not safety claims or cleanup. The pressure is close, though: IU Bloomington restricted all 27 of its IFC chapters in Nov 2025 [[6]](https://www.ipm.org/news/2025-11-17/iu-limits-frat-activities).
- Many HQs are in Indiana, and their foundations give their fraternities large education grants (FY2025): Lambda Chi Alpha (Carmel) $2,325,752 [[7]](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/lambda-chi-alpha-educational-foundation,136266432/), ZTA (Carmel) $861,898 [[8]](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/zeta-tau-alpha-foundation,310987111/), Delta Tau Delta (Indianapolis) $719,619 [[9]](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/delta-tau-delta-educational-foundation,311020203/), Kappa Alpha Theta (Indianapolis) $487,603 [[10]](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/kappa-alpha-theta-foundation,366066531/). That money already pays for their own staff and programs; it is not a software budget. Neither Lambda Chi nor Delta Tau Delta has a chapter here [[15]](https://life.indianapolis.iu.edu/get-involved/fraternities-sororities/index.html). Two local IFC chapters do have Indiana HQs: Alpha Sigma Phi in Carmel [[87]](https://alphasig.org) and Delta Sigma Phi in Indianapolis [[88]](https://www.deltasig.org).
- Members pay per head for risk: Lambda Chi's risk management assessment averages $224 per member per semester, and the page doesn't say how much of it is insurance [[11]](https://www.lambdachi.org/parents/). SAE's national HQ (Evanston, Ill., not Indiana) lists financing chapter insurance risk among its programs; its total revenue was $9.61M (FY2025), with no insurance breakdown [[12]](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/sigma-alpha-epsilon-fraternity,361773950/).
- National insurance excludes hazing, and breaking risk rules can void it [[13]](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2023/08/21/hazed-excused-some-national-fraternities-shift-financial-responsibility-hazing-deaths-through-self-created-insurance-system/). Risk policies ask for guest lists and sober event monitors, for example Alpha Omicron Pi [[25]](https://www.alphaomicronpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Risk-Management-Policy-2024.pdf). We found no source for a monitor-per-guest ratio; ask SAE's risk chair what its national requires.
- NIC, the fraternities' umbrella group, runs an "Enhanced Health & Safety Standards" pilot of its own [[86]](https://nicfraternity.org/about). A 2019 Sigma Nu magazine story about an event app called SocialSafe [[14]](https://www.sigmanu.org/the-delta/magazine/v134n2/higher-education/steps-in-the-right-direction) is no longer online, so we couldn't confirm an earlier event-app pilot or who ran it.
- Locally: 24 chapters in 4 councils, 5 of them IFC: Alpha Sigma Phi, Delta Sigma Phi, FIJI, SAE and TKE [[15]](https://life.indianapolis.iu.edu/get-involved/fraternities-sororities/index.html) (another IU page lists 7 [[16]](https://record.indianapolis.iu.edu/experiences/interfraternity-council-executive-board.html)). ### 2b. Insurers and housing corporations

**What we found**

- The old "FIPG" guidelines were dissolved by 2016; cite NIC and NPC standards [[24]](https://studentaffairs.lehigh.edu/sites/studentaffairs.lehigh.edu/files/offices/ofsa/docs/policies/7D-2019%20What%20Happened%20to%20FIPG.pdf).
- A typical policy (Alpha Omicron Pi, 2024): a guest list for every social event and any event with alcohol, at most 2 guests per member (a 2:1 ratio), event monitors including one officer, a licensed vendor or BYOB, no co-hosting with bars, no pooled money for alcohol, even through apps [[25]](https://www.alphaomicronpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Risk-Management-Policy-2024.pdf). Vibe can run guest lists. It must never take money for a chapter social event (tickets, door fees or "donations"), because that can count as pooled money for alcohol [[25]](https://www.alphaomicronpi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Risk-Management-Policy-2024.pdf).
- Holmes Murphy insures all nine NPHC (historically Black) groups. Members are covered only when acting within the scope of their organization, and each group has its own event-registration process, 21-30 days ahead for several [[26]](https://www.holmesmurphy.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Holmes-Murphy-Fraternal-Practice-NPHC-Organization-Insurance-Coverage.pdf). MJ Sorority in Carmel asks for 2 weeks' notice [[27]](https://mjsorority.com). No insurer we checked lists a software partner [[28]](https://www.holmesmurphy.com/industries/fraternal/holmes-murphy-fraternal-practice-partners/).
- SigEp's 2024-25 insurance bill is $2,350 per chapter plus $167.95-$376.87 per member [[29]](https://sigep.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-2025-Bill-Estimates-for-Budgeting.pdf), and SigEp gave insurance credits for using its own tools (2021) [[30]](https://sigep.org/wp-content/uploads/Spring-2021-Insurance-and-Member-Safety-Bill-Fee-Structure.pdf). Phi Kappa Psi (Indianapolis) adds 10% to a chapter's liability premium when it misses standards, one of which is a yearly Safe Social Events Plan sent to the national (2021-22) [[31]](https://www.phikappapsi.com/?p=8351). Neither has a chapter here [[15]](https://life.indianapolis.iu.edu/get-involved/fraternities-sororities/index.html), and using an app doesn't by itself meet those standards. Dollar amount at stake for a local chapter: not worked out (we'd need SAE's real premium).
- Our source for "no on-campus Greek housing at IU Indianapolis," a 2020 student blog, no longer says so [[32]](https://pearson.com/en-us/higher-education/insights-and-events/students-blog/2020/02/connecting-campus-greek-life.html), and no on-campus housing wouldn't rule out off-campus chapter houses. Those would have house corporations (alumni groups that own houses), a payer Kaki named. House corporations look small: one Indiana example (Franklin, not Indianapolis) reported no rental income, took in about $59,000 and spent $74,348 in FY2024 [[33]](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/237400586).

## 3. What this means for the product now

1. **The game card.** The server reads the feed at most every 2 hours, never `/services/`, and converts its UTC times. Plain-text names, no logos, "going" as a count with our usual floor. Off until athletics and IU Licensing and Trademarks both say yes in writing. No new personal data.
2. **Chapter event tools, in two steps.** First: registration with reminders and a guest list with the 2-guest cap. After the lawyer answers: QR check-in, sober-monitor roles, a chapter-owned export. **Privacy flag:** guest lists hold names of people who may not use Vibe, and check-ins record who came. "Chapter-owned" records still sit on our servers, so a court, the police or the school can demand them from us. Before these tools ship, decide how long lists are kept, what we do with a legal demand, and how deletion stops once a dispute starts, and change the Privacy Policy the same day. Nobody outside the chapter sees attendance; an HQ or insurer gets counts at most, because detailed records could be used to void members' coverage [[13]](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2023/08/21/hazed-excused-some-national-fraternities-shift-financial-responsibility-hazing-deaths-through-self-created-insurance-system/).
3. **"Sponsors" on event pages,** with no money at first.
4. **A "Sponsored" label and ad rules before any paid card:** deals open to all, no alcohol, no school logos, counts-only reports, Terms and Privacy Policy wording first.
5. **A safety card** linking to IU's module and reporting form.

Not now: a rewards app, attendance proof for the school, anyone's personal data going to nationals or the school, any payment that could buy alcohol.
