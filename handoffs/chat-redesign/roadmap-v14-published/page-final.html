Chapter 2 · What we all say

One sentence, one kind of club, three phrases.

Three different stories means clubs hear about three different apps. Main focus now: write down the one pitch all three of us say, then repeat it until we're sick of it.

The sentence

Vibe is where IU Indy and Purdue Indy students find their clubs, their events, and each other, all in one place.

Short: "Your campus, all in one place." We track each part: clubs (students following 3+ clubs), events (RSVPs), each other (friends and messages). Rylan tests it on five students by Sun Oct 4.

Tagline, picked (Franky, Sept 28): "Your campus. Your people. Yourself." It already shows in link previews and on the share image.

The first club: three yes/no traits
  • An event at least every two weeks.
  • Recruiting this fall.
  • Already posts on Instagram, so "nobody sees our posts" is real to them.

Start with clubs that say yes to all three, whatever kind. At least 3 of the first 10 are Purdue. "Replace Instagram" is our goal, never our pitch: officers say yes to lose a problem, not to join a dream.

Three phrases we always use

"Clubs first, then students." · "We build your page in front of you." · "We only claim what's live."

The pitch workshop, 25 minutes · planned for Sept 26, result not written down yet

The goal: one pitch all three of us can say word for word. Recommendation: if we wrote it on Sept 26, put it here before the first demo day; if not, run these 25 minutes at the first Sunday meeting, Oct 4.

  • 5 min, alone, phones down. Each of us writes three sentences answering a club president who asks: "What is Vibe, and why should we bother?"
  • 5 min, read them out. Underline every phrase that shows up twice. Those are the keepers.
  • 10 min, build it from the parts. The three problems we fix (empty events, posts nobody sees, the account mess when officers graduate). What we do in the room (build your page and load your events in 15 minutes). What it costs (nothing, and nobody quits Instagram). The sentence last.
  • 5 min, stress-test it. Rylan says it to Franky playing a doubtful club president, who asks: Who can see our member list? Can someone fake being a student? What if someone posts something awful? Can we remove a follower? What happens when I graduate? Whatever stumbles gets rewritten on the spot.

Then, at every real club meeting, we ask before we show. Five questions, the same every time: How do members hear about events now? What went wrong at your last event? Who decides which apps the club uses? What happens to your accounts when officers graduate? Would the club ever pay for anything here? The answers go in the scoreboard's "what we heard" tab.

Chapter 9 · The paperwork

A tax ID (the EIN), a bank account, a lawyer, two store accounts.

James runs the tax ID, the bank and the lawyer. Franky runs the store accounts. No bank, no Vibe+ sales. No lawyer-checked Terms, no store review. No store accounts, no apps.

The bank: Mercury first

  • $0 a month, no minimum, opened online, usually approved within two business days. All three of us get access; Franky gets an admin role to connect Stripe.
  • Needs the EIN letter, the founding papers, a real street address (a home address is fine, no PO box), and a photo ID from everyone named on the LLC.
  • Backup: Indiana Members Credit Union. Free checking, a branch on the IU Indy campus, $50 to open plus a $21.42 certificate from the state, probably in person.
  • Stripe pays out to either. The first payout takes 7–14 days and can't be sped up.

What not to do

  • Never pay for an EIN or a "BOI report." The EIN is free at irs.gov, and US companies no longer file BOI reports. Anyone charging is a scam.
  • No Vibe money in a personal account, Venmo or Cash App, ever. Mixing money can make us personally responsible for Vibe's debts.
  • Don't put anyone's name on a form until the three of us and the lawyer agree. The IRS, the bank and Stripe must all match.
  • No logins in group chats. Add people as users.
Two things nobody had mentioned

If two or more people are named on the LLC, it files a tax return every year, even at $0 revenue: federal by March 15, 2027, Indiana by April 15. Late costs about $260 per person per month. The EIN's "responsible party" is whoever actually runs the LLC, not whoever filed it, and changing it later means another form within 60 days. Both are on the lawyer's list.

The store accounts

Apple, as a company: $99 a year. Needs the D-U-N-S number, a work email on connectvibe.app, a public site naming the company, and an enroller who can sign for the LLC. Franky enrolls. The state filing names only James today, so James amends it by Sat Oct 3 to add Franky and Rylan as co-founders ("governing persons"), or the lawyer puts Franky's authority in writing before Oct 7. Apple approvals have taken days to a month this year, which is why Oct 9 is a decision day. Google Play: $25, once. It needs the D-U-N-S number too. Our running costs (hosting, tools, the domain) are about $125 a month, or about $150 with Supabase Pro (recommended, Chapter 12).

How does a club get its page?

Today: a student makes the club page from their own account and runs it. Recommendation: keep that, and add the club's official email. An officer makes the page from their own verified account, so every post has a real person behind it. At least two officers run it, and it passes to new officers at graduation. The club's school email earns a "verified club" badge and recovers the page if every officer leaves. No shared passwords.
