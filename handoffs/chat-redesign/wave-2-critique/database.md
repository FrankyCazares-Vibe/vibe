Counts: 0 HIGH, 3 MEDIUM, 11 LOW.

**The top 3 changes**

1. **Name the locks** (M1). `CREATE TABLE … REFERENCES public.messages / channels / users` takes SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on each referenced table until COMMIT (writes wait); the behaviour probe runs inside that window. **DATABASE-M1. The lock list of 3.10 is short: the four foreign keys hold SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on `messages`, `channels` and `users` until COMMIT, and the behaviour probe runs inside that window.**
- Evidence:
  - Draft 3.10: "Locks: `CREATE TABLE`, `CREATE FUNCTION` and `CREATE TRIGGER` on `channel_members` (a short `SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE` on `channel_members`)". Nothing about the referenced tables.

Adding a foreign key (inline in `CREATE TABLE` or by `ALTER TABLE`) takes SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on the REFERENCED table for the rest of the transaction (Postgres docs, "Explicit Locking": ADD FOREIGN KEY; the repo already writes this class of note for a trigger, `20260929120000_post_tags_rules.sql:106`). A held its ACCESS EXCLUSIVE locks over the same shape and said so (A:194-199, "nobody reads or writes chat meanwhile"); B's is milder (reads proceed) but longer if the probe grows.
  - The ROLLBACK's `drop table public.chat_poll_votes, …` also touches the referenced tables (dropping a FK removes its RI triggers on the parent: SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on `messages`, `channels`, `users` again; Postgres source, `RemoveTriggerById` / `ATExecDropConstraint`; labelled uncertain on the exact mode, the Mac's `pg_locks` read during the rehearsal settles it).
- Change, 3.10 and the header's APPLY ORDER section: "Locks: the five `REFERENCES` in 3.1 hold SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on `messages`, `channels` and `users` from step 1 to COMMIT (chat sends, RSVP-side `users` writes and sign-ups queue; reads and RI checks proceed); `CREATE TRIGGER` the same on `channel_members`; `lock_timeout 5s`, a quiet hour, Franky's yes. **DATABASE-M3. The contract does not say which of the INSERT policy's gates the RPCs give up, so a reviewer cannot tell a deliberate omission from a forgotten one.**
- Evidence:
  - `messages_insert_org_member` WITH CHECK, eight terms (A:1409-1418): `auth.uid() = user_id`; `kind = 'text'`; `card_event_id is null`; `media_url/media_kind null`; `removed_* null`; `can_post_org_channel(c.id, auth.uid(), thread_root_id)`; `has_recorded_consent()`; `can_publish_now()`.

  - Why it is safe today, also unwritten: the first statement of every RPC refuses `anon` and `authenticated` with 42501 (3.3:265-267), so the only callers are the routes with the service key, and each route runs its gates first.
