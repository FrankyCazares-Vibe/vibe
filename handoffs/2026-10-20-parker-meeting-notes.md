# Meeting with Parker (IU Innovates), Oct 20, 2026: notes

Franky's notes, as he sent them. Parker is IU Innovates' Founder-in-Residence (he ran IU's university-wide
incubator and founded Soloist, a consumer mobile app). What Franky took from it: **"Something that stood
out to me is the clubs view of this app. We need to design that."** That design is the next build step

## Vibe: background and evolution

- Started as a LinkedIn competitor for students to show personality over professionalism
- Built out heavily: feed, job posts, campus map, clips, group chats
- Sam flagged it was doing too much: "treat your customers like babies"
- Showcased at IU Innovates; unprepared on ICP and monetization
- Burnt out over summer (2 AM to 3 AM nights), stepped away entirely
- Returned this semester with a tighter focus

## Current direction: campus in your pocket

- Core problem: IU Indianapolis students feel disconnected despite 330+ student orgs
  - Events posted across five platforms; students still miss them or find out too late
  - Low turnout at school, club, and athletic events
- Tagline: "a campus in your pocket": one place for clubs, events, friends, and campus activity
- Parker reframed the root problem: loneliness and wanting to belong, not just missing event info
  - Students want connection first; event discovery is the mechanism

## Product demo and UX feedback

- App has: profile, feed, campus event board, club pages, group chats, event RSVPs, officer-only channels
- Parker's main feedback:
  - The social, LinkedIn-style feed is not the core value; events and club updates are
  - Users open the app with one specific goal at a time: check club news, find an event, etc.
  - Feature sprawl dilutes the experience, especially with a small early user base
  - Facebook analogy: started as "see who's in my Harvard class", nothing more
- Recommendation: map the user journey for a specific persona and remove everything not on that path
  - e.g. a pickleball fan should get from opening the app to pickleball club events in seconds

## Go-to-market

- Plan: onboard 20 clubs before opening to general students
  - Franky is in a fraternity and on the IFC board, giving direct access to Greek chapters
  - Kaki Gerard flagged fraternities spend heavily on anti-hazing content; a potential niche
- Parker endorsed the club-first approach
  - Gym membership analogy: sell to people already going to the gym, not non-exercisers
  - Clubs want to engage existing members; Vibe can be the tool, members become users
- Campus partnership angle: pitch schools to publish events on the platform
- Current blockers:
  - All costs out of pocket; needs a business bank account
  - Still onboarding clubs
  - Unsure on marketing direction (Parker: let clubs market for you at this stage)
- Parker recommended Mercury or a similar startup-friendly online bank (no fees for small accounts)

## Customer discovery

- Parker's core advice: stop talking only to already-involved students; go broader
  - Talk to 10 random people at a general campus event
  - Ask: how do you hear about events? How often do you go? How often do you wish you had?
  - Look for trends: sports vs. social vs. academic interest clusters
- Suggested user test: 2 club owners + 2 general students, 30-minute sessions. Pitch it, then watch them
  try to reach their goal unassisted
- Franky just got into the IU incubator; weekly Thursday meetings starting now
  - Sebastian (mentor) built a community platform himself; Parker strongly recommended leaning on him
