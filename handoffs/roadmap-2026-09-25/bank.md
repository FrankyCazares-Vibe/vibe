## Three things every bank will ask first

Sort these out at tomorrow's meeting. Every bank, the IRS, and Stripe will ask the same three things. **Give all of them the same answers.**

1. **The EIN.** Nothing opens without it. See the steps below.
2. **A real street address.** Right now the LLC's state record says the principal office is literally "NONE." Banks want a real street address. Mercury and Relay turn down PO boxes, UPS Store boxes, and registered-agent addresses. Mercury does take a home address. Pick one founder's **stable** home address (a parent's house works). Avoid a dorm you'll move out of in May.
   - This address is **not public**. The address we print in the Terms can still be a PO box later. That's a different job.
3. **Who the LLC's members are.** By law, banks have to check the ID of everyone at or above a set level in the LLC (25% under the federal rule, 10% at Chase), plus one person in charge. The IRS EIN form also asks **how many members** the LLC has. That answer changes how the LLC files taxes. This isn't written down anywhere yet. **It's a question for the lawyer, not this doc.** Don't guess on a bank or IRS form.

## Why Mercury is the first pick

1. **$0 forever for what we need.** No monthly fee, no minimum balance, free ACH and wires in US dollars. Chase costs $15 a month until we keep $2,000 sitting there. We have $0 in revenue.
2. **No branch trip.** Chase makes all three of us show up at a branch together. Mercury is an app on each person's phone. That matters with three class schedules.
3. **It fits three co-leads.** Unlimited team members with roles: James runs it, and Franky and Rylan can see every dollar. Bluevine and Novo are built around one owner.
4. **Fewer papers up front.** Mercury's own list is formation documents + EIN + photo IDs. Relay needs a signed operating agreement first.
5. **Made for Stripe businesses.** Plenty of startups send Stripe payouts into Mercury, and there's a Mercury Stripe app.
6. **Safe.** Money sits at Mercury's partner banks (Choice Financial Group and Column N.A.), both FDIC insured.

**The honest downside:** it's an app, not a bank you can walk into. You can't deposit cash. Vibe doesn't take cash, so that's fine.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't pay anyone for an EIN.** The IRS says it plainly: "You never have to pay a fee for an EIN." Sites with "irs" or "ein" in their name that charge you aren't the IRS.
- **Don't pay for a "BOI report."** US-formed LLCs no longer file one. FinCEN made that permanent in August 2026. Scam letters mention "Form 4022" or "Form 5102," and neither form exists.
- **Don't take Vibe money into a personal account, Venmo, or Cash App.** If business and personal money mix, a court can ignore the LLC and come after your personal stuff.
- **Don't use a PO box, UPS Store box, or registered-agent address** as the bank address. Mercury and Relay will reject it or lock the account.
- **Don't put member names or numbers on any form (IRS, bank, Stripe) that the three of you and the lawyer haven't agreed on.** These are legal statements, and all three places have to match.
- **Don't apply at three banks at once** with different answers.
- **Don't open Chase first** "because it's familiar." It's $15 a month until we keep $2,000 in it, and all three of us have to be in the branch together.
- **Don't send account numbers or logins in a group chat.** Add people as users instead.
- **Don't lose the EIN letter.**

## Gotchas for student founders

- **Addresses move.** Dorms and leases change every year, and Mercury limits the account if the address on file is wrong. Use a stable address, and update it the day you move.
- **Everyone must be 18+** with a US ID and SSN. Banks check each person.
- **Class schedules vs. branch visits.** Chase wants every member there at the same time. Apps avoid that.
- **Credit checks.** IMCU says accounts are "subject to credit approval." Thin credit history is normal at 20. If one of us gets flagged, ask what they need instead of giving up.
- **An app is not a bank.** Mercury, Relay, Bluevine, and Novo hold our money at partner banks. That's still FDIC insured, but support is chat and email, not a branch.
- **Taxes: the big one.** By default, the IRS treats an LLC with two or more members like a partnership. It then files a yearly return (**Form 1065**) even with $0 in revenue. The 2026 return is due **March 15, 2027**. Late filing costs about **$255 per member per month** (TaxAct; **UNVERIFIED** on irs.gov). Indiana may have its own version (**UNVERIFIED**). **Put this on the lawyer or an accountant's list now.**
