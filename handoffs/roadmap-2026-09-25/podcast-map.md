## 1. The five biggest ideas, framed for Vibe

1. **Clubs say yes to get rid of a problem, not to join a dream** *(Grosser)*. So the pitch to a club officer starts
   with the problems: empty events, posts nobody sees, accounts lost when officers graduate. Then it takes the risk off
   the officer: it's free, we build the page in the room, we load their events, and nobody has to quit Instagram. The
   meeting should be worth the officer's time even if they say no.
2. **Growth comes from the people already here** *(Sellis)*. Make Vibe great for the first 10 clubs and their members
   before inviting the whole campus. Measure it by how many days out of 7 those students open Vibe, not by sign-ups.
3. **One sentence, one number, and the taste to cut** *(Sellis)*. Agree on one sentence for why a student opens Vibe
   tomorrow. Split it into parts, give each part a number, and hide every screen that doesn't serve it. AI tools add
   features and almost never remove them, so the cutting is Franky's job.
4. **Signing clubs is a process to write down and improve every week** *(Grosser)*. That means:
   - a written playbook,
   - one scoreboard,
   - a log of what clubs and students say, with a rule that we build a request only after 3 different people ask
     for it,
   - one fix to the pitch every week, the way Franky fixes bugs.
5. **A small team moves only as fast as its slowest handoff** *(both)*. So:
   - one named owner decides each outcome,
   - Franky hands work over for real but still sits in on club meetings to listen,
   - credit is given by name, out loud.

## 3. How we work: a charter for Sept 26

Seven lines to agree on tomorrow. Each one has a real opposite some teams choose, which is the test for a principle
that actually does something (Sellis).

1. **Every Sunday we meet for 30 minutes, with the same agenda.**
   - Scoreboard first (5 minutes).
   - The one or two things that broke in how we sign clubs, and who fixes them by next Sunday (10 minutes).
   - Decisions owed (10 minutes).
   - Each of us names next week's one result, plus one specific thing each of the other two moved forward (5 minutes).

   Franky runs the meeting and sends notes that night.
2. **Franky posts a 3-line note every weekday morning:** today's priority, who owns it, and what's blocked. When a date
   breaks, he re-plans that day, not at the next meeting.
3. **We own outcomes, not tasks.** Each of us has one October result with our name on it:
   - **James:** Terms live and the bank open.
   - **Rylan:** 10 clubs live with upcoming events.
   - **Franky:** Vibe in students' hands on Oct 31.

   How you get there is your call. Nobody redoes anyone else's work.
4. **One owner decides.** In your own area, you hear the other two once, then decide. We don't reopen it, and we back
   the call in front of clubs, the lawyer and students. Disagreements happen in the founders' chat, never in public.
   If a decision crosses two areas and we're stuck for 24 hours, Franky decides and writes down why.
5. **Ask early and be specific.** The day you see a date slipping, say "I need X from Y by Z." Raising a problem early
   is never a failure. A surprise on the deadline is.
6. **Done means someone outside the three of us can see it.** For example: live on connectvibe.app, a club page with a
   real event, a form actually submitted, a letter in the shared folder, a number on the scoreboard. "Drafted" and
   "almost" are not done. And we only claim what's true, in every pitch, post and number.
7. **More ownership, not more hours.** When someone delivers, they get the next bigger decision. Classes come first,
   and nobody burns out for Oct 31.

Lines 1, 2 and 4 are what makes this Franky's leadership, not just a group of three. He runs the rhythm, he writes
first every day, and he makes the tie-breaking call in writing. He also sits in on one demo day a week, as a listener,
not the lead (Grosser).

## 4. Tensions and what I recommend

**Where the episodes push against the current plan**

1. **Franky's October hours.** The store apps are Franky's priority, but both episodes say the founder's attention
   belongs with the first customers. Today there is 1 real club, 0 upcoming events and 0 Purdue students.
   **Recommendation:** keep the store apps (students expect them, and phone notifications live there), but limit what
   they cost. Oct 9 decides both stores with no extensions (R15). Franky listens in on one demo day a week. Store work
   never borrows Rylan's or James's hours.
2. **"Replace Instagram."** It's a dream pitch, and Grosser says most people act to avoid a problem, not to chase a
   dream. It's also a very wide target, and Sellis says Discord won by narrowing and that Instagram even copied Snap's
   failures. **Recommendation:** keep it as our own long-term goal and never say it in a club pitch. After launch, add
   Instagram-style features only when students say they'd use them on a campus app (R8).
3. **A daily habit, or weekly events?** Sellis says a consumer app has to earn daily use. Vibe's hooks are weekly
   (events) or one-time (customizing your profile). Customization is a reason to sign up, rarely a reason to come back
   tomorrow. **Recommendation:** answer one question at the meeting: *what does a student open Vibe for on a day with
   no event?* The likely answer is friends' posts, messages and "tonight on campus". Turn push notifications on at
   launch as the daily reason to come back, and count days out of 7 (R17).
4. **Vibe+ as a student subscription.** Sellis thinks subscriptions grow slowly and that ads suit consumer apps better.
   Grosser says to watch who actually pays and change the plan to match. Our own belief is that seeing *who* viewed you
   is what people will pay for. **Recommendation:** nothing changes for launch. Vibe+ stays off and hidden in the
   stores. Free Vibe+ as a thank-you for club officers and ambassadors is fine, as long as they say so in their posts
   (see `ambassadors.md`). James compares the options in November (R22), and we decide in January.
5. **Launch targets.** `marketing.md` aims for 250 sign-ups by Oct 31. Sellis says to grow how often the people already
   here come back. **Recommendation:** at the Oct 23 go/no-go check and the Nov 1 look-back, lead with clubs live and
   weekly active club members. Sign-ups come second and are never the headline.

**Where the research files disagree with each other**

6. **Ambassadors in October?** `ambassadors.md` recruits about 10 in October. `marketing.md` (citing a16z) says
   ambassador programs work best once people already come back. Grosser says to write the playbook before others
   pitch, and Sellis says to start with the core. **Recommendation:** in October, ambassadors are only officers of clubs
   that have already signed. Recruit them after the playbook (Wk 2) and after the lawyer's answer about unpaid perks.
   Other connectors (RAs, orientation leaders) wait until mid-November and a real weekly-return number.
7. **Four scoreboards.** The files plan four trackers on three different days with two owners. **Recommendation:** one
   sheet (R9). James owns it, it's filled in by Friday and read on Sunday. Franky sends counts only, which keeps
   "only Franky can open the database" true.
8. **Two different one-sentence pitches.** `marketing.md` and `podcast-product.md` each propose one. On top of that,
   Sellis says to tell the same story every time, while Grosser says to pitch clubs their problem. **Recommendation:**
   use one sentence, `marketing.md`'s, for students, posts and the store listing. Clubs hear their 3 problems first and
   the same sentence last.
9. **Many kinds of clubs vs. 3 traits.** The plan includes Greek, faith, cultural and Purdue clubs, and `ambassadors.md`
   wants a mix. Grosser says a small team can handle about 3 traits, not five groups. **Recommendation:** make the
   traits about how a club behaves, not what kind of club it is. Then Greek, faith and cultural clubs can all qualify.
   Keep the floor of 3 Purdue clubs.

**Where the two episodes disagree with each other**

10. **Leave people alone, or stay close?** Sellis cuts down on collaboration and pushes his best people to their limit.
    Grosser builds one connected customer path, keeps the founder in the room, and mixes backgrounds so people learn
    from each other. **Recommendation:** use both. There's one shared path from "never heard of Vibe" to "posts every
    week", with one owner for each step. Give more ownership, not more hours, since we're unpaid students with
    classes. Franky listens at demos but never takes over or redoes the work.
11. **How much to lean on AI.** We build with AI all the time. Grosser automates only after a person has done the job
    well, and she keeps a human sending every message. Sellis warns that AI adds and people have to cut.
    **Recommendation:** send outreach by hand first (R16), and make the shelf list (R13) before the store screenshots.
