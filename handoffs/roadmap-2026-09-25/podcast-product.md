## 1. Who he is and the big idea

Peter Sellis was Snapchat's first product manager and stayed seven years, then became Head of Product at
Discord, where he helped drive some of its fastest growth since the pandemic. He's between jobs now, so he
spoke freely. His big idea: a small team wins when everyone shares one simple belief about why people come
back every day and everyone knows exactly who decides what, and growth mostly comes from making that core
thing better for the people already using it and having the taste to say no to good ideas that don't serve it.

## 2. Lessons for Vibe

### 1. Know who decides what, so you don't have to meet about everything
**The lesson.** Peter builds teams on two things: a belief everyone shares, and a very clear map of who
is trusted with which decision. He thinks working together has a hidden cost nobody counts. When
everything needs everyone, "you're moving as slow as the slowest node in the system" (Peter Sellis). He
also says every role has to add real value compared with nobody doing it.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** This is the first time Vibe's work is split three ways. The Oct 31 plan is a
chain where each step waits on the one before (LLC answer, then the D-U-N-S number, then Apple and Google,
then the store apps). If all three of you weigh in on every step, the launch goes only as fast as whichever
of you is busiest that week.
**The move.** At Saturday's meeting, write a one-page "who decides" list. **James decides** which lawyer,
the LLC details and the bank. **Rylan decides** which clubs to approach and in what order, how demo days
run, and what the teasers say. **Franky decides** what's in the app and what ships. The other two give
input once, the owner decides, and nobody reopens it. Name each job by a result only that person makes
happen. James: Terms live by about Oct 14. Rylan: 10 clubs, each with a page and upcoming events. Franky:
store apps sent in by Oct 16, or the website app ready if Apple isn't done by Oct 9.
**Owner:** all three (Franky brings the first draft). **When:** Sat Sept 26.

### 2. Write one sentence for why people come back every day, and pair it with a number
**The lesson.** At Snap and Discord, Peter's teams had what he calls "core product value." It's one short
sentence about why someone opens the app every day, split into parts, with a number for each part. Snap's
was about being the fastest way to share a moment with your closest friends. "Fast" meant load time,
"share" meant the app opens straight to the camera, and "closest friends" meant who you send to. Discord's
was about talking and hanging out with friends before, during and after games. Every roadmap idea got
checked against the sentence. You need both halves: "If it's just metrics, it's kind of meaningless to
people" (Peter Sellis). And a sentence with no number never changes what anyone builds.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Vibe has a mission (replace Instagram for students, orgs and clubs). It doesn't
have a sentence that says why a student opens it *tomorrow*, or a number that shows whether it's working.
Right now, 1 account has signed in during the last 7 days.
**The move.** Agree on a draft sentence Saturday. Here's a starting point to argue with: *"The easiest way
to see what your campus is doing, and go with your people."* Its parts: **see what your campus is doing**
(club posts and upcoming events), **go** (RSVPs), **with your people** (friends and clubs you follow). The
number: out of the last 7 days, on how many did a student open Vibe and see something new from a club or
friend they follow? Rylan tries the sentence on 5 students. Franky sets up a simple Monday count from the
database before launch.
**Owner:** Franky drafts, all three agree, Rylan tests it, Franky sets up the count. **When:** draft Sept
26, tested week of Sept 28, count ready week of Oct 19.

### 3. Turn the plan into short phrases and repeat them until they're boring
**The lesson.** Peter took the founders' vision and boiled it down into short phrases people could repeat,
like "long-term greedy" (see lesson 12). At Snap he gave new hires the same talk about the core sentence
every week or two, for seven years. A phrase keeps making the case when you're not in the room.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Rylan and James will be the ones explaining Vibe to club officers, the lawyer
and students, often without Franky there. If each of you tells a different story, clubs hear about three
different apps.
**The move.** Rylan writes a 2-minute demo-day pitch built on the lesson-2 sentence, plus 2 or 3 phrases
the team always uses (for example, "clubs first, then students" and "we build your page in front of you").
All three of you should be able to give it word for word. Use the same phrases in the Instagram and TikTok
teasers.
**Owner:** Rylan (Franky reviews it once). **When:** week of Sept 28, before the first demo day.

### 4. Growth comes from the core, not from chasing new groups
**The lesson.** Peter learned this twice: growth mostly comes from making the product better for people
already using it for its main purpose. At Discord, everyone assumed every gamer was already on it. The
team focused only on friends playing games together anyway, and moved people from about 10 days a month to
11, then 12, then 13. He calls that huge growth. Flashy side stories (like the AI art app Midjourney running
on Discord) barely showed up in the usage numbers.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Before launch, Vibe's core is the first 10 clubs and their members. Today
there's 1 real club (SAE). Its only follower is a founder, and clubs have posted 0 times. If you invite the
whole campus onto empty club pages, you waste every student's first impression.
**The move.** Make SAE's page the model: real posts and at least 2 upcoming events before the next demo
day, so every other club sees what "done" looks like. After launch, judge each week by how many days out of
7 club members open Vibe, not by new signups, and fix whatever stops them from coming back.
**Owner:** Rylan (SAE and the clubs), Franky (the weekly number). **When:** SAE ready week of Sept 28;
weekly check starts week of Nov 2.

### 5. Speed is a feature, especially on the phones your users actually carry
**The lesson.** Snap's worst moment was a 2018 redesign that stalled its growth. The fix wasn't a new
feature. The team made the app much faster for people already using it, especially on Android, and that
kicked off years of growth.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** The Android app has never been built. My guess (worth checking) is that a
commuter campus like IU Indy and Purdue Indy has more students on older or mid-range Android phones than
the founders' own phones suggest. If the app is slow the first time a student opens it on launch day,
Vibe probably loses that student.
**The move.** Before sending the app in, borrow a mid-range Android phone from a student and time three
things: opening the app, loading the feed, and opening a club page. Fix anything that feels slow before
adding new features.
**Owner:** Franky. **When:** week of Oct 12 (before the Oct 16 submission).

### 6. Taste is mostly saying no
**The lesson.** Peter watched Snap's founder turn down ideas that were better than what most startups ever
launch. The way he sees it, a great product is like a museum that puts only a small part of its collection
on the walls. If everything is hung up, "the curator hasn't done anything" (Peter Sellis). He tests
people's taste by asking what they built but chose not to ship. He and Lenny point out that AI tools
always add and hardly ever cut, so the cutting is now a human job.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Vibe has been built fast with AI help, and it has a lot on the walls: the
feed, profiles and customization, clubs, events, messaging, the map, the campus assistant panel, resumes,
and Vibe+. A new student gives it about 30 seconds.
**The move.** Before the store screenshots are taken, Franky makes a "shelf list": every screen a new
student can reach, each marked keep or shelf. Anything that doesn't serve the lesson-2 sentence gets hidden
for launch (hidden, not deleted). Rylan brings what students said in interviews, and Franky decides.
**Owner:** Franky. **When:** week of Oct 5.

### 7. Don't copy Instagram's whole playbook. Copy only what your students use
**The lesson.** Peter says Instagram copied Snapchat so hard that it copied some of Snap's worst features,
ones that weren't even working, because it assumed everything Snap touched was working. What Instagram
got right, like stories, worked because Instagram rebuilt it to fit its own app.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** "Replace Instagram" makes it tempting to rebuild Instagram's feature list.
Instagram has thousands of engineers. Vibe has one builder and five weeks. A feature is only worth copying
if students on this campus will actually use it on Vibe.
**The move.** In student interviews, Rylan asks one question about each Instagram-style feature on the
wish list: "Would you use this on a campus app, or only on Instagram?" Only features that get a real yes
go on Franky's build list for after launch.
**Owner:** Rylan asks, Franky decides. **When:** weeks of Sept 28 and Oct 5.

### 8. Give more to whoever is delivering, and back each other in public
**The lesson.** Peter's rule: when someone keeps making good calls, "I just want to load them up with more
decisions" (Peter Sellis), until he starts to see bad ones. He always defends his people in public and
never criticizes them in front of others. He warns that not checking on someone doesn't mean they're
failing. With unusual, brilliant people (he managed Nikita Bier), you put them where they're great. He
compares people to bands: if you still believe in the artist, stick with them through a weak album.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Franky wants James and Rylan to own real work. Owning means the decision is
theirs, not doing tasks Franky hands out and then redoes. And club officers, the lawyer and Apple will all
notice whether the three of you act like one team.
**The move.** Each cofounder gets one area Franky won't overrule (see lesson 1). When someone nails
something (say Rylan signs his first 3 clubs), hand him the next bigger thing (say, running Vibe's
Instagram). Have disagreements in the private founders' chat, never in front of a club, the lawyer or a
student. One caution: Peter pushes his strongest paid employees until they nearly break. You're all
students with classes, so give more ownership, not more hours.
**Owner:** Franky. **When:** from Sept 26.

### 9. Map your launch in a spreadsheet
**The lesson.** Peter says most of what people call "systems thinking" is just asking "why?" five times.
The useful version is writing the system down with numbers: what comes in, what goes out, and which
levers matter. Even if the spreadsheet never leaves the shared drive, it makes your thinking clearer.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** The launch is a chain: clubs signed, then club pages and events, then
students invited, then students who follow a few clubs, then students who come back the next week. Right
now nobody knows which step is weakest, or what "good" looks like on Oct 31.
**The move.** James builds a one-page launch spreadsheet with a target for each step (for example, 10
clubs, 20 upcoming events, X students invited, Y% following 3 or more clubs). Rylan fills in the real
numbers every Friday. The sheet is the first 5 minutes of every weekly meeting.
**Owner:** James (Rylan supplies the numbers). **When:** built week of Sept 28, updated every Friday until launch.

### 10. Watch for students going out of their way. That's where to grow next
**The lesson.** Peter calls it one of the greatest gifts: people using your product in a way you didn't
plan, and jumping through hoops to do it. On Discord, people stretched a chat app built for small friend
groups into big communities. They were "voting with their feet" (Peter Sellis), and Discord built for it.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Demo days and the first weeks after launch will show you what clubs and
students really want, better than any plan can. But those signs get lost if nobody writes them down.
**The move.** Rylan keeps a simple "workarounds log." Every time a club or student does something the
long way or asks "can it do X?", he writes one line with the date and who. Examples: posting a flyer photo
because an event field is missing, or asking to message only members. Franky reads it every Monday and
picks at most one thing to build.
**Owner:** Rylan logs, Franky picks. **When:** from the first demo day; Monday reviews after launch.

### 11. Fix your pickiest users' small complaints, and say so out loud
**The lesson.** Peter says gamers were the most critical users he'd ever built for. Discord told them to
point at the bug threads, and said "We don't care how small it is, how big it is, we will fix it" (Peter
Sellis). Keeping that promise earned deep loyalty.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** College students notice every glitch, and the first club officers are doing
Vibe a favor. Fixing what they point out quickly, and telling them it's fixed, is how 10 clubs turn into 30.
**The move.** Before launch: if a club officer points out a problem at a demo day, fix it within 48 hours
when you can, and Rylan tells them it's done. After launch: a short weekly "you told us, we fixed it" post
on Vibe's own account and on Instagram.
**Owner:** Franky fixes, Rylan tells the club. **When:** from the first demo day; weekly posts from the
week of Nov 2.

### 12. Be "long-term greedy": help clubs first, charge later
**The lesson.** In ads, Peter's teams used the phrase "long-term greedy." Tomorrow's money comes from the
value you give customers today, so give that value even if it costs you money in the short run. Having a
name for it gave the team cover to think long-term when the pressure was on.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Clubs are probably Vibe's best future payers (clubs have budgets, students
mostly don't), but only after Vibe proves it brings people to their events. Charging them or pushing
upgrades before that would burn the relationship.
**The move.** This semester, every club gets everything free. After each club event, Rylan asks the
officer one question, "Did anyone come because of Vibe?", and writes down the answer. That record becomes
your proof when you ask clubs to pay.
**Owner:** Rylan asks; James owns the question of when to charge and what for. **When:** from the first
posted event; revisit pricing after the semester.

### 13. Subscriptions come after the habit, and they grow slowly
**The lesson.** Peter says that with subscriptions, "time is your enemy" (Peter Sellis). Nobody opens
Snapchat for the first time wanting to pay. People pay only after they've been using the app daily, and
the share who pay creeps up slowly. He wishes Snapchat+ had launched years earlier for exactly that
reason. He also thinks ads, not subscriptions, are the natural way for a consumer app to make money.
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Vibe+ ($3.99 a month) is Vibe's only paid plan. It's switched off until the
tax ID, the bank account and the Terms are done. Even once it's on, don't count on real money from it for
months.
**The move.** James adds a money tab to the lesson-9 spreadsheet. It shows how many paying students it
would take to cover costs if growth is slow, next to a second option: clubs or local businesses paying to
promote events. Keep any Vibe+ pitch out of sign-up and out of a student's first weeks.
**Owner:** James (Franky keeps the paywall out of sign-up). **When:** after launch, week of Nov 9.

### 14. Lead by setting the pace and giving specific credit
**The lesson.** Peter says a great product leader holds three pairs of opposites. The first is confident
but humble: they hand out credit by name, since the leader gets too much credit anyway. The second is
organized, with good process, but fine throwing the plan out. The third is patient about the long game
but urgent every day: "When they wake up in the morning, the company wakes up" (Peter Sellis).
**Why it matters for Vibe.** Franky wants to show he's serious about leading. A steady pace and specific,
public credit show that far better than a title does.
**The move.** Every weekday morning, Franky posts a 3-line note in the founders' chat: today's one
priority, who owns it, and what's blocked. Every Friday, he names one specific thing James and one
specific thing Rylan moved forward. When the plan breaks (say Apple isn't done by Oct 9), he re-plans that
same day instead of hanging on to the old plan.
**Owner:** Franky. **When:** starting Mon Sept 28.

## 3. How we work: principles to adopt

Peter's test for a principle: if nobody could disagree with it, it isn't doing anything. ("Collaborate
well" fails that test.) Each principle below has a real opposite that some other teams choose.

1. **One owner per decision.** The other two give input once, the owner decides, and we back the call in
   public. *(The opposite: decide everything together.)*
2. **Every feature serves our one sentence, or it waits on the shelf.** Shelved means hidden, not
   deleted. *(The opposite: ship everything we've built.)*
3. **The people already here come first.** Make Vibe better for the clubs and students already on it
   before chasing new ones. *(The opposite: chase signups.)*
4. **Long-term greedy.** Clubs and students get real value before anyone is asked to pay. *(The
   opposite: make money early.)*
5. **Same story, every time.** One sentence and a few phrases, repeated until we're sick of them. *(The
   opposite: pitch it fresh for every audience.)*

## 4. Where the episode argues against Vibe's plan (honestly)

1. **The daily habit is missing.** Peter says almost every consumer app has to earn daily use. People
   live their lives in days, and much less in weeks or months. Vibe's hooks are weekly (events) or
   one-time (customizing a profile). Customization is a good reason to sign up, but it's rarely why
   someone opens an app the next day. Right now there have been 0 messages and 1 sign-in in the last 7
   days. The question for Saturday: **what does a student open Vibe for on a day with no event?** If the
   answer is friends' posts and messages (the Instagram part), then that has to be great at launch, not
   only the club pages.
2. **The money plan leans on the model he trusts least.** Peter thinks subscriptions are overrated for
   consumer apps and that ads are the proven way to make money. Vibe+ is a student subscription. Two
   things work in Vibe's favor. First, users are 18 and up, so Vibe avoids the strict ad rules for
   under-18 users that hurt Snap's ad business. Second, Vibe has feeds and event pages, which are natural
   places for promoted club events or campus-area businesses. (Snap opens to a camera, with no natural
   place for an ad.) Don't change anything for launch, but the post-launch money plan should compare
   both. An earlier Vibe note already suggested that clubs may be an easier first sale than students.
3. **A lot is running at once.** The Oct 31 plan now has an iPhone app, an Android app that's never been
   built, notifications, payments (off), moderation, and an 8-step paperwork chain where each step waits
   on the last. Peter's point about the slowest person setting the pace applies: one late answer moves
   everything. The Oct 9 decision day is a good safety valve. His advice would push you to make that call
   early, and not let the store apps pull hours away from what the numbers say matters most: the first
   10 clubs.
4. **"Replace Instagram" is a very wide target.** Discord won by narrowing to one use (friends playing
   games), making it perfect, and only then growing outward. Instagram hurt itself by copying Snap's
   failures. The episode would back the narrow version of Vibe (clubs and friends on this one campus). It
   warns against the wide version (all of Instagram, for students).
5. **Some of his advice doesn't fit a team of three students.** His habit of pushing his best people as
   hard as they can go is meant for paid staff. Pushing unpaid cofounders with classes that hard risks
   losing them. His doubts about collaboration mean fewer decisions made by everyone, not less talking.
   And a lot of the episode (hiring product managers, how OpenAI should run ads) is about big companies
   and isn't useful to Vibe right now.
