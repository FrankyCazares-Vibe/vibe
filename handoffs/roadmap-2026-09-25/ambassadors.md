# Vibe Founding Ambassadors: a no-money plan for October 2026

Owner: **Rylan**. Helpers: Franky (app side), James (money and lawyer questions).
Written 2026-09-25 for the founders meeting on 2026-09-26. Launch deadline: **Oct 31, 2026**.

---

## The short version

1. **Recruit about 10 students, not 50.** Most should be officers of the clubs we sign. A few more are "connectors": Greek chapter reps, cultural org leaders, orientation leaders.
2. **Their main job is getting their own club onto Vibe.** Bringing friends and telling us what's broken come second.
3. **Pay them in thank-yous, not cash.** Free Vibe+, early access to new features, a real resume line with a written reference, a say in what we build, and a public shout-out.
4. **Track three numbers in one private sheet:** clubs live on Vibe, students who joined through an ambassador, and how many of those students came back a week later.
5. **Follow the school rules first.** IU and Purdue both ban most on-campus advertising for companies (flyers, chalk, tabling) unless the school approves it. Week 1 is asking the school what we can do.

The main lesson from other apps: **paying students per download gets you a spike, not a habit.** Groups that already meet, and leaders people already listen to, are what made campus launches stick.

## What other apps did, and what we can learn

| App (year) | What they did | What happened | Lesson for Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facebook** (2004) | Harvard only at first, then a few schools picked on purpose. You only saw people at your own school. | 5,055 Harvard students by March 1, 2004. Zuckerberg said the site was about "the people you are likely to meet when you walk outside your dorm." [1] | One campus, packed full, beats many campuses half-empty. Vibe already works this way because you need a school email to join. |
| **Tinder** (2012–13) | Whitney Wolfe presented at sorority chapter meetings and had everyone install the app in the room. Then she went to the fraternity they paired with. [2] | Under 5,000 users before her trip, about 15,000 after. [2] | Go to groups that already meet. Get people signed up **in the room**. Visit paired groups back to back. |
| **Yik Yak** (2013–14) | The founders emailed campus leaders. Reps handed out stickers and drink koozies. [3] | Cofounder Brooks Buffington: once one person passed it along through their email list, "we would take over the campus." It later fell apart over bullying and threats. [3] | **A leader with a mailing list is worth more than a flyer.** Safety has to be ready before growth. |
| **Snapchat** (2011–12) | No ambassadors. One Orange County high school picked it up because the school iPads blocked Facebook. [4] | About 20,000 users in Jan 2012, about 100,000 by April 2012. [4] | Ambassadors can't create a reason to use the app. Vibe's reason is **every club's events in one place**. Lead with that. |
| **Fizz** (2021–) | Free donuts for downloads, paid student promoters, and student moderators at every school. [5][6][7] | 95% of Stanford undergrads downloaded it. [5] By 2024: 240 colleges and 4,000 volunteer moderators. [6] At the Claremont Colleges it hit its goals at 3 of 5 schools. Flyers in dorms felt like "an invasion of privacy" to students. [7] | Moderators are part of the launch, not an extra. Dorm flyers can backfire. |
| **Sidechat** at Northwestern (Oct 2022) | Paid ambassadors $15 to $100 to hand out cookies and post on Instagram. [8] | 1,412 downloads in 2 days. Then an ambassador said they unfollowed and deleted their posts once they were paid. "Now, I don't think anyone uses it." [8] | **Paying for posts buys a spike.** We want people who actually like Vibe. |
| **BeReal** (2022) | Paid ambassadors about $30 per referral. Parties where getting in meant downloading the app and adding 5 friends. [9] | Became huge in 2022, then daily users dropped sharply in 2023 (reported by Contrary Research). [9] | Referrals get people in the door. Only the app itself keeps them. |
| **Red Bull, Gopuff** (ongoing) | Paid, professional programs. Gopuff gives each ambassador a referral code, a weekly task list (posts, group messages, tabling) and a 12-week season. [10][11] | These run every year at big schools, including IU Bloomington this fall. [11] | Copy the **structure**: one code per person, a short weekly checklist, a season that ends. Skip the paycheck; we don't have one. |
| **Robinhood** (2013) | A waitlist where referring friends moved you up the line. [12] | Reportedly about 1 million signups before launch (from growth blogs, not Robinhood itself). [12] | Seeing where you stand on a list makes people want to climb it. A simple leaderboard works. |

Not found: I couldn't find a solid source on how Handshake used student reps early on. Its growth went through school career centers, not students. **UNVERIFIED**, so I left it out.

## Who to recruit

Pick people other students already follow. In order:

1. **Officers of clubs that sign onto Vibe.** They're already in our club pipeline, and they have a reason to care: their events get seen. Aim for one per signed club.
2. **Greek chapter reps.** Fraternities and sororities are tight, meet every week and throw events. Tinder's lesson: sign up a sorority and its paired fraternity together.
3. **Cultural and faith org leaders.** Big, loyal groups. Faith-based orgs are already in our launch group.
4. **Orientation leaders and peer mentors.** They know lots of first-years. **Rule:** they help as regular students, never in their official school role.
5. **RAs (resident assistants, who live on dorm floors).** Same rule: never use RA group chats, floor boards or their RA title. Their employer (Residence Life) probably has its own rules. **UNVERIFIED**, so Rylan asks before recruiting any RA.

Aim for a mix: IU and Purdue students, different majors, and at least 3 first- or second-years. Upperclassmen graduate; younger students keep the network going next year.

## How many

- **Target 8 to 12 people for the first group ("the Founding 10").** One startup guide says one lead plus 2 or 3 ambassadors is enough to reach the right groups at first. [13] We have more clubs to reach, but three founders can't manage more than about 12 people well.
- **Rylan is the lead.** Every ambassador talks to Rylan, not to all three founders.
- Pick them by personal invite, not a public job post. Ask each yes: "who else should we talk to?" [13]

## What an ambassador does each week (about 2 hours)

| Task | How much | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Get their own club onto Vibe (page built with a founder there, events posted) | Once, then keep events updated | Clubs come before students. An empty campus feed is the biggest risk. |
| Introduce Rylan to **one** more club leader they know | 1 per week | Leader-to-leader intros beat cold messages (the Yik Yak lesson). |
| Bring friends who'd actually use it | About 5 real students a week, no pressure | Real people, not numbers. |
| One honest post or story about Vibe, with the disclosure (see Rules) | Up to 1 per week | Teasers are already part of our marketing plan. |
| Ask 3 students what they think, and write it down | 3 short chats a week | Their feedback steers what Franky builds. |
| Fill in the 2-minute weekly form | Every Sunday | Lets us track it all without meetings. |
| 15-minute group check-in with Rylan | Weekly | Wins, problems, the next week's focus. |

Everything is optional and flexible. No shifts and no minimum hours (see the lawyer question under Rules).

## Tracking it simply

**Right now the app has no way to record who invited whom.** I checked the code: there's a "Share Vibe" button in sign-up, but nothing that credits the person who shared. So:

- **Start with no build (Week 1).** When a friend joins, the ambassador writes that friend's Vibe username in their weekly form. Rylan looks the account up in the app to check it's a real, school-verified student.
- **Later, a small build, only if Franky wants it:** a personal invite link per ambassador (for example, the sign-up link ending in `?ref=maria`) that the app remembers at sign-up. It's Franky's call; the phone apps come first. If we build it, the Privacy page has to say we record who invited you.
- **Only Franky can see the database.** Once a week he pulls one number for the sheet: of the students who joined through ambassadors, how many came back after 7 days.

**One private Google Sheet, founders only.** Ambassadors never see each other's rows. They report through a 3-question weekly form.

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Ambassador (name, Vibe username) | Maria L., @marial |
| Club | Latino Student Association |
| Club page live? Events posted? | Yes, 2 events |
| Club intros this week | Pre-Med Society (meeting set for Oct 14) |
| Friends who joined (Vibe usernames only) | @x, @y, @z |
| Came back after 7 days (Franky fills in) | 2 of 3 |
| Feedback (short notes) | "Wants event reminders" |
| Rewards given | Vibe+ on Oct 8 |
| Rule problems | none |

**No emails or phone numbers in the sheet.** Usernames only.

**The three numbers that matter** (not follower counts or downloads):
1. Clubs live on Vibe with at least one upcoming event. **Goal: 10 by Oct 31** (already in our launch plan).
2. Real, verified students who joined through an ambassador. **Goal: 100 to 200 by Oct 31.** This is a guess to test, not a promise.
3. How many of them came back a week later. **This is the one that tells us if it's working.**

## The pitch (one paragraph, for DMs or in person)

> Hey [name], I'm Rylan, one of the three students building **Vibe**. It's a new social app just for IU Indy and Purdue Indy students: clubs, events, profiles and messages in one place, and you need a school email to get in. We launch at the end of October, and we're picking about ten students clubs already listen to as our **Founding Ambassadors**. It's about two hours a week: help us set up your club's page, bring friends you think would like it, and tell us honestly what's broken. It's not a paid job. You'd get Vibe+ free, first access to new features, a real line for your resume with a written reference from us, and a direct say in what we build next. Do you have 15 minutes this week so I can show you?

Every claim in it has to be true on the day it's sent. If something isn't live yet, leave it out.

## The Ambassador Promise (rules everyone agrees to in writing)

1. **Be honest.** Only say what's true today. Never say or hint that IU or Purdue runs or backs Vibe. It doesn't.
2. **Say you're an ambassador.** Free Vibe+ is a perk, so the law treats you as connected to us. Put it at the **start** of the caption, not in a hashtag or behind "more." Example: "I'm a Vibe ambassador (they gave me free Vibe+)." The FTC (the federal agency for honest advertising) says disclosures buried at the end or in comments are too easy to miss. The company also has to train and check on its ambassadors. [15]
3. **No spam.** No mass DMs to strangers. No posts in class group chats, Canvas or official school channels. At most one message about Vibe in any group chat you're really part of.
4. **No fake accounts.** Don't make accounts for other people, and don't borrow school emails. Faking it means you're out and the accounts get removed.
5. **No pressure.** "No thanks" is a fine answer. Never make club membership depend on joining Vibe.
6. **Respect privacy.** Never screenshot anyone's posts or messages to promote Vibe. Never share anyone's personal info.
7. **Follow school rules** (next section). No flyers, chalk, tables or dorm door-knocking unless we have a written OK.
8. **No IU or Purdue logos** in anything you make. IU Indianapolis doesn't let student orgs use its logos either. [17]
9. **Pass safety problems up fast.** If someone tells you about harassment or something unsafe on Vibe, send it to a founder the same day.

First slip: a quick talk with Rylan. Spam, fake accounts or lying: out of the program.

**Question for the lawyer (James), before recruiting starts in Week 2:** the federal "volunteer" exception covers non-profits. For-profit companies are judged by a "who benefits more" test. [16] Vibe is a company (an LLC). Are unpaid ambassadors who get perks OK if they have no set hours and every task is optional? Does free Vibe+ count as pay? **UNVERIFIED until the lawyer answers.** Until then: flexible, optional, and called a "founding members group," not a job.

## School rules we have to respect

- **IU policy FIN-PURCH-12, "Non-Solicitation on Campus"** (in effect since 2019-03-12, covers students): no advertising for a company on IU property. That includes chalking and handing out or posting printed ads, unless IU Procurement Services approves it ahead of time. [18]
- **Purdue policy IV.B.3** (revised 2016-08-15): marketing any product or service on Purdue campuses is generally banned. Recognized student orgs need written approval from the Dean of Students and Purdue's student-org business office. It covers "regional campuses." **UNVERIFIED** how it applies to Purdue students on the shared Indianapolis campus. [19]
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether a free app counts as "commercial." Vibe is run by an LLC, so we assume yes until the school says otherwise.
- **Probably fine** (these happen off school property): posts on your own social accounts, messages to friends, and a club officer telling their own club about a tool the club uses.
- **Needs a yes first:** flyers, chalk, tables, anything in the dorms, and a founder presenting at a club's meeting in a campus room. Asking about that last one comes first, because it's our best tactic (the Tinder lesson).
