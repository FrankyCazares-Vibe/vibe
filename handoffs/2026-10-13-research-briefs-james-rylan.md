# Research briefs for James and Rylan (drafted Oct 13, 2026; widened the same day)

Two research jobs that came out of Franky's meeting with Kaki. One each, and
each one is that person's to own from start to finish. The aim is a full
picture of a competitor, worked backwards: how they decided who their ideal
customer is, what that customer wants, what people will actually pay for, how
they sell it, and how well it has gone. In other words, reverse engineer how
they went to market.

Suggested due date: Sunday, October 18 (Franky to confirm).
Format: a few pages of plain notes with links to where each fact came from.

## The same questions for both

**The product**
1. What does it do for a student, and for a chapter or club officer?
2. What do students say about it (app store reviews, Reddit, people we know
   who have used it)? What does it do well and badly?

**The customer**
3. Who is their ideal customer: students, chapters, national organizations,
   universities, businesses? How can you tell?
4. What does that customer want badly enough to pay for? What problem are
   they buying a fix for?
5. Who uses it free, and who pays?

**How they sell**
6. What exactly do they sell, and at what price if it is public?
7. How do they reach buyers: sales calls, conferences, partnerships, campus
   reps, ads, word of mouth? What does their website and pitch lead with?
8. Where did they start (which campus, which organization), and how did they
   spread from there?

**How it has gone**
9. What proof of success is there: campuses, chapters, users, funding, press,
   named partners, how long they have been at it?
10. What has not worked, or what did they drop?

**What it means for Vibe**
11. What should we copy, and what should we avoid?
12. Where is Vibe clearly different, in one or two sentences?
