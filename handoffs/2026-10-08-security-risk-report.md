## Executive Summary

Vibe was reviewed for three kinds of risk: security, privacy, and money. The review looked at the places and questions that matter to a student: who can read whose messages and profiles; what happens to the photos, videos, and resumes students upload; how sign-in, sign-up, and password changes work; what an outsider with no account can see or collect; what the company would pay if one person set out to abuse the app; and whether the legal pages (Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Child Safety Standards) match what the app does. It also checked whether the live site tells browsers to apply the standard protections, which it does.

The method was reading, not attacking. The reviewers read the code, the database's own access rules (the list of who may read or change each table), the settings, and the outside software packages the app depends on, and five plain visits were made to the public site. The live database was asked only for counts, never for a person's record, as the Privacy Policy promises. No sign-in was needed and none was done. A second reviewer then tried to knock every finding down. Nobody attacked the live site, because real students' data and private messages are on it; anything only a live attempt could settle would be marked as needing a test on a copy of the site, and in this round nothing needed that. Of 57 possible problems raised, 20 held up as confirmed in today's code. None is rated Critical, one is High, and the rest are Medium or Low.

The honest picture: a student using Vibe today is not exposed to the things people usually fear. No one can read another student's private messages, club chats, or school email. No one can pull the student list straight out of the database, because the hardening pass that landed in early September, and the follow-up on September 22, turned on access rules for every table and moved every profile read behind the app itself. Resumes are private and are served through the app with contact details blacked out. What remains falls into four groups. First, a legal gap: if someone sends an illegal or intimate image in chat and the sender then deletes their account, the only copy of the evidence is erased, and the company cannot make the report the law requires. Second, account takeover at the edges: a password can be changed from a borrowed, unlocked laptop with no current-password check and no email alert, and the site lacks the browser rule that would contain the damage from a future code bug. Third, cost and outages: one person could stop every sign-up code from arriving, fill the company's storage with junk that is billed monthly, or run up the database bill, all for free. Fourth, privacy and paperwork: anyone without an account can copy the public student directory, a profile owner could learn who views their profile, and the live Terms of Service still calls itself a draft and names no company.

### Top things to do now

1. Keep a locked, separate copy of any chat image that was reported or removed by a moderator, kept even after the sender deletes their account, so the company can always make the report the law requires (closes #1).
2. Require the current password, or a fresh sign-in, before a password change, and email the student when it happens, so a borrowed laptop can no longer become a silent lockout (closes #2).
3. Add the browser rule that lists which code is allowed to run on Vibe's pages, keep the sign-in cookie out of page code's reach, and make sessions expire, so a future code bug becomes a nuisance instead of a full account takeover (closes #3 and #4).
4. Put a daily cap and a cooldown per address on every email the app sends, including the sign-in service's own emails, so one person can no longer stop sign-up codes for everyone (closes #5 and #6).
5. Cap the total upload permissions one account can receive per day, pin each upload to its declared file type, and run a nightly sweep that deletes uploads never attached to anything, so one account cannot run up the storage bill or host a fake page (closes #8, #9, and #10).
6. Add a request limit to the signed-out profile, post, and club pages and to the signed-in actions that have none, upgrade the web framework to its current patch (a one-line change), and replace the draft Terms with a real one naming CONNECTVIBE LLC and the right county, so directory copying and runaway database bills stop, a known hole is closed before the next feature opens it, and the terms hold up in a dispute (closes #7, #11, #13, and #14).

## Authentication & Session Security

This section is about signing in and staying signed in: how a student proves who they are, how Vibe remembers them between visits, how a password gets changed or reset, and whether anyone else could end up inside their account. The basics are sound. Passwords are checked properly, school-email verification works, sign-up codes are single-use, and the reset emails Vibe sends itself were built with care. The three problems below are all about what happens *after* a sign-in exists, and about settings on the sign-in service that are still at their factory defaults. None is open to a stranger today on its own; each turns a small slip into a lasting one.

### [Medium] A copied sign-in never expires, and nothing on the page stops it being copied  — confirmed in the live app today

**In plain terms:** If anyone ever manages to sneak their own code onto a Vibe page, they could copy a signed-in student's "remember me" and keep using that account from anywhere for as long as they like, because Vibe never makes an old sign-in run out. That includes the three admin accounts, so one such slip would let a stranger read private messages, post as students, and moderate the whole campus as if they were a founder. There is no known way to sneak code in today, but Vibe has no second lock on the door either: the only way a student ends the stranger's access is to sign out of every device or change their password, and nothing tells them to.

**What it is:** The browser Supabase client (`createBrowserClient` in `src/lib/supabase/browser.ts:11-16`) stores the GoTrue access token *and* refresh token in the `sb-rqopjkimxbjkwdkpkbfq-auth-token` cookie with `@supabase/ssr`'s defaults: `httpOnly: false`, `sameSite: lax`, `maxAge: 400 days` (`node_modules/@supabase/ssr/dist/main/utils/constants.js:4-11`). That is inherent to the SSR helper, so the real defences are (a) stopping script injection and (b) limiting what a stolen cookie is worth. Neither is in place. The live login page sends no Content-Security-Policy (anonymous `curl -D - https://www.connectvibe.app/auth/login` shows only HSTS, X-Frame-Options and nosniff; `next.config.ts`, `src/proxy.ts` and `vercel.json` set none). The desktop shells under `public/html/` run inline scripts that call `/api` with `credentials: include` (`public/html/_postViewer.js:1267,1572`). Production GoTrue has `sessions_timebox=0` and `sessions_inactivity_timeout=0` with refresh-token rotation on (reuse interval 10 s). Live counts: 69 sessions and 69 unrevoked refresh tokens for 21 users, so old sessions are never pruned.

**How an attacker uses it:** 1. Find any reflected or stored XSS on the origin (the `/html` prototypes with inline scripts are the softest target). 2. The payload reads `document.cookie`, reassembles the `sb-…-auth-token` chunks and POSTs the JSON (access_token + refresh_token) to the attacker. 3. The attacker calls `POST https://rqopjkimxbjkwdkpkbfq.supabase.co/auth/v1/token?grant_type=refresh_token` with the public anon key and the stolen refresh token roughly every 50 minutes; rotation hands them a fresh pair each time, and with no timebox or inactivity timeout the session lives until the victim does a global sign-out or changes their password. 4. They read DMs and club chats, post as the victim, and (next finding) set a new password to lock the victim out.

**Impact:** One XSS anywhere on the origin becomes a durable account takeover of whoever loads the page, including the three platform admins (the admin API checks only `users.is_platform_admin` for the session user). Exposed: DMs, club chats, profile viewers, school email. No direct dollar cost today (billing is off), but a compromised moderation-admin session is a platform-wide incident.

**What to do:** 1) Ship a Content-Security-Policy from `src/proxy.ts`: nonce-based `script-src 'self' 'nonce-…'`, `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'self'`; move the inline scripts in `public/html/*.html` into files so the policy can drop `'unsafe-inline'`. 2) Supabase → Auth → Sessions: set a time-box (e.g. 30 days) and an inactivity timeout (e.g. 7 days) so a stolen refresh token has a shelf life; consider `sessions_single_per_user` for admins. 3) Treat 69 sessions for 21 users as a signal: add a "Sign out everywhere" button (`auth.signOut({ scope: 'global' })`, which logout already uses). 4) Longer term, put the three admin accounts behind TOTP (GoTrue has `mfa_totp` enabled) and enforce `aal2` in `requirePlatformAdmin` by checking the JWT `aal` claim.

**Where:** `src/lib/supabase/browser.ts`; `src/proxy.ts` (no CSP); `public/html/*.html`, `public/html/_postViewer.js:1267,1572`; Supabase Auth → Sessions (`sessions_timebox`, `sessions_inactivity_timeout`); `auth.sessions` / `auth.refresh_tokens`.

### [Medium] A signed-in account can get a new password with no old password, no re-check and no warning email  — confirmed in the live app today

**In plain terms:** Anyone who gets even a few minutes inside a signed-in Vibe account, such as a roommate on an unlocked laptop or a library computer a student forgot to sign out of, can set a brand-new password without knowing the old one. The real owner is kicked out of every device, gets no email saying the password changed, and the intruder keeps the account until the owner notices and runs a password reset from their inbox. For a campus full of shared screens this is the most likely way a real student loses their account, and closing it is mostly a settings switch, not a rebuild.

**What it is:** Production GoTrue has `security_update_password_require_reauthentication=false`, and no password-changed notification is configured (the config exposes `mailer_notifications_email_changed_enabled=false` and no `password_changed` notification key at all). `/auth/update-password` deliberately supports a session-only mode, "no link at all: only someone already signed in … can set a password here" (`src/app/auth/update-password/page.tsx:475-485`), then calls `supabase.auth.updateUser({ password })` (`page.tsx:380`, `saveNewPassword` at `:352-386` with `tokenHash=null`) on whatever session the browser holds. Nothing asks for the old password. GoTrue then revokes every *other* session for that user (`LogoutAllExceptMe`), so the legitimate owner is signed out everywhere while the attacker keeps the only valid session. Secure email change *is* on (`mailer_secure_email_change_enabled=true`), which is what keeps this from being a permanent lockout.

**How an attacker uses it:** Precondition: any session access — the cookie path in the previous finding, a shared or unlocked device, a phone left signed in at a club table. 1. In the victim's session open `https://www.connectvibe.app/auth/update-password` with no token in the URL. 2. The page shows "Choose a new password for <victim email>" and the form. 3. Submit a new password; GoTrue accepts it with no nonce and no old password, saves it, and revokes the victim's other sessions. 4. The victim's app signs them out on the next refresh, they get no email, and the attacker continues as them until the victim runs a password reset.

**Impact:** Turns transient session access into exclusive control of the account for as long as the victim does not notice. Recovery is possible (inbox-based reset) because secure email change is on, but nothing alerts the victim to start it.

**What to do:** 1) Supabase → Auth → Email: enable "Secure password change" (require recent login / reauthentication). The recovery-link flow keeps working because the recovery session is fresh; an old session must re-prove via `/auth/v1/reauthenticate`, and `page.tsx:209-216` already has `REAUTH_COPY` for `reauthentication_needed`, so this is a config change, not a code change. 2) Enable the password-changed notification email (Auth → Emails → Notifications) so a victim learns within a minute. 3) In the app, require the current password (or a fresh reset link) on the session-only branch of `/auth/update-password` instead of accepting any live session. 4) On password change, also DELETE `push_devices` / invalidate other devices the way logout does.

**Where:** `src/app/auth/update-password/page.tsx:352-386, 475-485`; GoTrue `PUT /auth/v1/user`; Supabase Auth settings `security_update_password_require_reauthentication`, `mailer_notifications_*`.

### [Medium] Anyone can use up Vibe's hourly email allowance and block every new sign-up code  — confirmed in the live app today

**In plain terms:** Anyone on the internet can make Vibe's sign-in service fire off its own password-reset and sign-in-link emails to any Vibe address, no account needed, and that service may only send 40 emails an hour for the whole app. Burning those 40 blocks every new student's confirmation code for the rest of the hour, so during launch week one bored person with a short script could stop sign-ups cold and new testers would see "we can't send confirmation emails right now". The same trick fills a student's inbox with reset emails they never asked for, and the link in those emails lands on a page that does nothing, which reads as a broken product on day one.

**What it is:** The app sends its own password-reset mail through Resend (`admin.auth.admin.generateLink` + `token_hash`, `src/app/api/auth/password-reset/route.ts:15-17, 82, 95`) precisely so it can rate-limit per IP and per hashed email (`:35`, `:57`) and avoid scanner-burnable links. But GoTrue's own mailers are still reachable by any browser holding the public anon key: `POST /auth/v1/recover`, `/auth/v1/otp` (magic link), `/auth/v1/resend`, `/auth/v1/magiclink`. `external_email_enabled=true`, there is no captcha (`security_captcha_enabled=false`), the per-address cooldown is 60 s (`smtp_max_frequency=60`), and the project-wide cap is `rate_limit_email_sent=40` per hour. That cap is shared with the sign-up confirmation code, the only way a new student gets in (`signup-form.tsx` already maps `over_email_send_rate_limit` to "We can't send confirmation emails right now"). The dashboard recovery, magic-link and email-change templates still link `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}`, the exact pattern the repo abandoned because Microsoft 365 link scanners GET it and spend the token, and the recovery link lands on `site_url` (`https://www.connectvibe.app/`) with `#access_token=…`, which no page there consumes.

**How an attacker uses it:** Budget exhaustion (launch-week DoS): with one known Vibe address (their own, or any founder/tester address) run `curl -X POST https://rqopjkimxbjkwdkpkbfq.supabase.co/auth/v1/recover -H "apikey: <anon key>" -d '{"email":"…"}'` once a minute, or against a handful of addresses in parallel; after 40 sends GoTrue returns `over_email_send_rate_limit` to every sign-up for the rest of the hour. Repeat hourly for the whole of launch week. Harassment variant: point the same calls at a specific student's address so they receive an unsolicited reset or sign-in-link email every minute, each with a link that goes to a page that does nothing. Scanner variant: any `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}` mail delivered to an @iu.edu / @purdue.edu inbox may have its token spent by the mail scanner before the student clicks, so GoTrue-sent links fail silently.

**Impact:** Sign-ups and school-email verification blocked for ~100 testers during the one week that matters, with no server-side log that distinguishes abuse from demand. Unwanted emails to students, which is both a trust hit and a complaint path. No data exposure, no money, but a cheap, repeatable way to make the first impression "Vibe doesn't work".

**What to do:** 1) Move GoTrue onto custom SMTP (the existing Resend account) in Supabase → Auth → SMTP and raise `rate_limit_email_sent` well above 40; the default cap only exists because the built-in mailer is shared. 2) Enable captcha on GoTrue (`security_captcha_enabled`, hCaptcha or Turnstile) so `/recover`, `/otp`, `/magiclink` and `/signup` need a captcha token; the app's own sign-up and sign-in forms must then pass `options.captchaToken`. 3) Since the app owns password reset, point the dashboard recovery template (and magic-link / email-change) at `{{ .SiteURL }}/auth/update-password?token_hash={{ .TokenHash }}&type=recovery`, the same `token_hash` pattern as `password-reset/route.ts:95`, so a scanner GET cannot spend the token and the link lands on a page that handles it; or, if magic links stay unused, turn them off. 4) Add a per-IP limiter in `src/proxy.ts` for any client-side call paths you keep, and watch GoTrue's `over_email_send_rate_limit` count in logs during launch week.

**Where:** Supabase Auth → Rate limits (`rate_limit_email_sent`, `smtp_max_frequency`), Auth → Email templates (Recovery, Magic Link, Change Email), Auth → Captcha (`security_captcha_enabled`); `src/app/api/auth/password-reset/route.ts`; `src/app/auth/signup/signup-form.tsx` (`over_email_send_rate_limit` mapping); GoTrue `POST /auth/v1/recover`, `/otp`, `/resend`, `/magiclink`.

## Authorization & Data Isolation

This is about who is allowed to see and change whose information: whether one student can look at another student's profile, follow list or club roster when the app says they should not, and whether a club's members and officers can only do what their role allows. The good news is that the big walls hold: nobody can read another person's private messages, email, or resume, and the club rules we set out to confirm (only officers see or remove followers, hidden clubs stay invisible, only officers send invites, a student can only edit the text, tags and draft status of their own posts) are all in place. What remains are three small gaps, all with the same shape: the app's screens enforce a rule that the database underneath does not, so a determined person who talks to the database directly can step around what the app shows them.

### [Low] A student you blocked can follow you again and ping your notifications — confirmed in the live database today

**In plain terms:** If a student blocks someone who is bothering them, the app stops the follow in both directions and promises the blocked person can no longer reach them. But the blocked person, with a little technical effort, can re-add themselves as a follower anyway, which drops a fresh "so-and-so followed you" notice into the victim's feed and quietly adds them back to the victim's follower count. The victim has to block them again each time, and the harasser can repeat it as often as they like. This breaks the one promise a block is supposed to keep, which is exactly the moment a student decides whether Vibe feels safe.

**What it is:** Blocking is enforced only in the route. `/api/me/follow` refuses a follow when a `blocks` row exists either way (`src/app/api/me/follow/route.ts:71-89`) and `/api/me/block` tears down both follow edges (`src/app/api/me/block/route.ts:165-182`). But `authenticated` still holds INSERT on `public.connections` (live grant confirmed today: DELETE,INSERT,SELECT) and the live INSERT policy `connections_insert_follower` is only `WITH CHECK ((auth.uid() = follower_id) AND (NOT is_restricted_now()))` — no `is_blocked_either_way`, no `has_recorded_consent()`. There is no BEFORE INSERT trigger on `connections`; the only trigger is `trg_notify_on_connection` (AFTER INSERT → `notify_on_connection_insert()`), which inserts a `follow` notification for `following_id` unless an older follow notification from the same actor still exists. The Session-51 pass (`20260928120000_blocks_in_rls`) added block checks to posts, comments and join-request policies but not to this table.

**How an attacker uses it:**
1. Victim V blocks attacker A in the app; both follow edges are deleted and every route refuses A's reads of V.
2. A copies their own session access token from the browser (the `sb-*-auth-token` cookie or devtools) and runs:
   `curl -X POST https://rqopjkimxbjkwdkpkbfq.supabase.co/rest/v1/connections -H 'apikey: <anon key from the bundle>' -H 'Authorization: Bearer <A JWT>' -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"follower_id":"<A>","following_id":"<V>"}'`
3. The row is accepted (the policy only checks `auth.uid() = follower_id`). The trigger writes a `follow` notification into V's Otto panel ("A followed you") unless one already sits there; V's follower count includes A again. V cannot delete the edge on A's behalf (`connections_delete_follower` is `auth.uid() = follower_id`) and must repeat the block, which A can undo again at will. No per-key rate limit applies to direct REST calls.

**Impact:** The block promise in the ToS and Privacy Policy (a blocked person cannot contact you) is defeated for the follow relationship: a harasser can keep re-appearing as a follower, re-ping the victim's notifications, and skew the victim's public follower count. An account that never accepted Terms can also create follow edges this way, because the policy has no consent check and the route's consent gate is bypassed.

**What to do:** Make the database the boundary, as the security model says. Either (a) `ALTER POLICY connections_insert_follower ON public.connections WITH CHECK (auth.uid() = follower_id AND NOT is_restricted_now() AND has_recorded_consent() AND NOT is_blocked_either_way(auth.uid(), following_id))`, or (b) `REVOKE INSERT ON public.connections FROM authenticated` and have `/api/me/follow` insert with the service client after its existing checks (the pattern already used for `org_followers`). As belt to that brace, add a BEFORE INSERT trigger that raises `23514` when a `blocks` row exists in either direction, and make `notify_on_connection_insert()` skip blocked pairs. Record in the migration that `/api/me/block` already deletes both edges, so no backfill is needed. Apply it via the management API with curl and add the `schema_migrations` row.

**Where:** policy `connections_insert_follower` and trigger `trg_notify_on_connection` on `public.connections`; `src/app/api/me/follow/route.ts:71-89`; `src/app/api/me/block/route.ts:151-182`; compare `posts_select_authenticated`, `post_comments_select_authenticated`, `org_join_requests_select`, which already call `is_blocked_either_way`.

### [Low] A student you blocked can still watch your public profile — confirmed in the live database today

**In plain terms:** When you block someone, the app shows them "Profile unavailable" and your page disappears from their searches and lists. But the same public details the app hides (your photo, headline, major, year, the free-text "location" you wrote, your website, your interests, your new username if you rename yourself to get away from them, even how many people have viewed your profile) can be pulled straight from the database by that blocked person. Your posts stay hidden, and nothing private like your email or resume leaks. Still, a student who blocked someone to escape them would reasonably expect that person to lose the window onto their profile, and right now they do not.

**What it is:** The live catalog shows `authenticated` holds SELECT on 23 columns of `public.users` (avatar_url, banner_gradient, banner_url, campus_id, created_at, department, handle, handle_changed_at, headline, id, interests, location_text, major, name, pinned_post_id, profile_view_count, school, school_system, school_verified, tagline, website, work_order_manual, year; e.g. `20260916100000` grants school_system/campus_id). The SELECT policy `users_select_authenticated` is `id = (SELECT auth.uid()) OR user_visible(id)` (confirmed live today), and `user_visible()` only checks `account_restrictions`. There is no block condition. Every API surface — profile bootstrap, `/api/users/by-id`, search, followers/following/mutuals lists — deliberately answers "Profile unavailable" or 404 for a blocked pair so nobody learns they were blocked, but the same data is one REST call away. This also corrects the recon note that said `authenticated` has no column grants on `users`: it has the public-profile columns; only the private columns (email, personal_email, is_platform_admin, last_active_at, resume_*, otto_*) are correctly server-only.

**How an attacker uses it:** Attacker A, blocked by victim V, runs with their own JWT:
`GET https://rqopjkimxbjkwdkpkbfq.supabase.co/rest/v1/users?handle=eq.<V handle>&select=id,name,avatar_url,banner_url,headline,tagline,major,year,campus_id,school,location_text,website,interests,pinned_post_id,profile_view_count,handle_changed_at` with `apikey: <anon>` and `Authorization: Bearer <A JWT>`.
The row comes back. A can poll it by the stable `id` to watch V's avatar, headline, `location_text`, new handle after a rename, and profile-view count, while the app tells A the profile is unavailable. V's posts stay hidden because `posts_select_authenticated` does check `is_blocked_either_way`.

**Impact:** The block feature's privacy promise is partly hollow: a harasser keeps a live view of the victim's public profile fields, including a free-text location and handle changes (a victim who renames to escape is found immediately via the stable `id`). The in-app concealment ("this person blocked you" is never revealed) is also reconstructable: the route says unavailable, REST says present. No private columns are exposed.

**What to do:** Add the block to the policy, asymmetrically so the blocker's own Blocked list still resolves names: `users_select_authenticated` → `id = auth.uid() OR (user_visible(id) AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM public.blocks b WHERE b.blocker_id = users.id AND b.blocked_id = auth.uid()))`. The `blocks` subquery needs a SECURITY DEFINER helper like the existing `is_blocked_either_way`, or a one-directional sibling `is_blocked_by(users.id)`. Before shipping, grep the client bundles and `public/html/**` for direct `from("users")` reads that could render a blocker (mentions, @-search, `hydrateUserCards`) and move any that must keep working to a service-role route. Also correct the recon/handoff note about `users` column grants.

**Where:** policy `users_select_authenticated` and column grants on `public.users`; `user_visible()`; `src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:205-240` (`loadPairBlock` → "Profile unavailable"); `src/app/api/users/by-id/[id]/route.ts:52-58`; `src/app/api/users/[handle]/followers/route.ts:74-86`.

### [Low] Any signed-in account could read the full member list of an open club — confirmed in the live rules, but no club is set up this way yet, so nothing is exposed today

**In plain terms:** The app treats a club's roster as something only its members get to see; everyone else is told "Members only". The database underneath is more generous: for any club that is public and open to join, it would hand the complete member list, each person's role and the day they joined, to any signed-in account, including someone from the other university, someone whose school email was never verified, or someone every member has blocked. Today this affects nobody, because no club is currently set up as public and open (SAE is invite-only and the test clubs are hidden). The first time a club opens its doors, the gap becomes real, and a club officer who was promised a members-only roster would be right to feel misled.

**What it is:** `/api/orgs/[slug]/members` is members-only by design ("A roster is the members' list of each other", `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/members/route.ts:82-93`): it looks the org up with the service client, re-checks `hidden_at` and membership itself, and returns 404 for a hidden club and 403 `members_only` for a non-member. The live RLS policy `org_members_select` is wider (confirmed today): `is_org_member(org_id, auth.uid()) OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM orgs o WHERE o.id = org_members.org_id AND o.is_public AND o.join_policy = 'open' AND o.hidden_at IS NULL)`, and `authenticated` holds table-level SELECT on `public.org_members` (live `relacl`: DELETE,MAINTAIN,REFERENCES,SELECT,TRIGGER,TRUNCATE). The route and the policy disagree about who may see a roster; for a REST caller the policy wins. Live count today: 0 orgs with `is_public AND join_policy = 'open' AND hidden_at IS NULL`, so the wider rule currently matches no rows.

**How an attacker uses it:** With any session JWT: `GET /rest/v1/orgs?select=id,handle&join_policy=eq.open` to find open clubs, then `GET /rest/v1/org_members?org_id=eq.<id>&select=user_id,role,joined_at&order=joined_at` to pull the whole roster with roles and join dates. Join the `user_id` values to `users` (the previous finding: public-profile columns are readable) for names, handles and avatars. None of the route's checks (verified school email, same university, block state, membership) apply on this path.

**Impact:** For every open public club, the complete membership with officer roles and join dates is readable by anyone who can sign in, while the app tells the same person "Members only". Blocked people and students from the other school can enumerate who is in which club. Nothing beyond the roster leaks (no messages, no channels), and the fix is a one-line policy change with no app code involved.

**What to do:** Make the policy say what the route says. Preferred: `REVOKE SELECT ON public.org_members FROM authenticated` and let the members route keep serving the roster through the service client it already uses (grep `src/` and `public/html/**` first for any direct `from("org_members")` reads that would break, e.g. a "you are a member" check, and move them to a route). Alternative: `ALTER POLICY org_members_select ON public.org_members USING (is_org_member(org_id, auth.uid()))`, dropping the open-public branch. In the same migration, clean up the stray table privileges on `org_members` for `authenticated` (MAINTAIN, REFERENCES, TRIGGER, TRUNCATE are not reachable through PostgREST, but they should not be there): `REVOKE MAINTAIN, REFERENCES, TRIGGER, TRUNCATE ON public.org_members FROM authenticated`. Ship it before the first club flips to `join_policy = 'open'`.

**Where:** policy `org_members_select` and table grants on `public.org_members`; `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/members/route.ts:63-100`.

## Injection, XSS & Input Handling

This is about what happens when a student types something into Vibe and it later shows up on someone else's screen or in someone else's calendar: can the text or link they entered be turned into something harmful for the person viewing it. We looked at every place user-typed text is painted onto a page, every link a student can set, and whether any private keys or passwords leak into the code the browser downloads. The overall state is sound: the Session 51 escaping holds, no secrets reach the browser, and nothing here lets one student run code on another student's screen. Two gaps remain, both about links and text that leave the app and get opened elsewhere, one of them a real privacy leak that contradicts what the Privacy Policy promises.

### [MEDIUM] A profile owner can secretly see who looked at their profile — confirmed in the code today

**In plain terms:** A student can find out exactly who opened their profile, and when, without the viewer ever knowing. They do it by putting a small picture link on their own profile (a company logo under "Work" or "Working on", or an outside résumé link) that points at a computer they control. Every time another student opens that profile, the viewer's phone or laptop silently asks that computer for the picture, and the owner's log now shows the viewer's internet address and the exact time. If the owner shares a slightly different profile link with each person, they can tell which named person looked. Vibe's rule is "counts are public, identities are private", and the Privacy Policy says a profile view does not reveal the viewer. This quietly breaks both. A student who learns their crush, their RA, or their ex can see them looking at a profile stops trusting the app and stops coming back.

**What it is:** Session 51 pinned `avatar_url`, `banner_url` and `banner_gradient` to Vibe's own Supabase host precisely because a URL painted on other students' screens is an IP beacon (comment block at `src/app/api/me/profile/route.ts:63-83`, migration `20260912102000`). The same class is still open for three profile fields that visitors render: `work_experience[].logo_url` (`sanitizeWorkExperience` in `src/lib/profile/work-experience.ts:25-50` accepts any `http:`/`https:` URL and stores `u.href`), `current_on[].logoUrl` (`sanitizeLogoUrl` in `src/lib/profile/current-on.ts:20-34`, same check), and `resume_docs[].url` / `resume_url` (external hosts allowed by design per `src/lib/profile/resume-doc-url.ts:13-19`; `/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:305-327` only hides an external doc when redaction bars are out of step). Sinks: desktop visitor page `public/html/profile.html` renders them in `<img src="…">` after `_esc` (current-on 10325-10329, work logo 10347-10350, résumé thumbs/pages 5887-5896 and 5938-5943) and `_rasterizePdf` fetches an external PDF URL inside the visitor's browser via pdf.js `getDocument({url})` (5100-5111). Phone: `src/components/mobile/ProfileMobile.tsx:5160` paints `url(${logoUrl}) center/cover` into a React style; `src/components/mobile/ResumeViewerMobile.tsx:799-800` renders `<img src={pageUrl}>`. There is no CSP with an `img-src` directive (grep of `next.config.ts` and `src/proxy.ts` finds none), so nothing stops the request. This is not script execution: the `<img>` context and `_esc` hold.

**How an attacker uses it:** (1) Edit own profile normally; add a "Working on" item or a work role whose logo URL is `https://attacker.example/pixel.png?v=1` (or paste an external résumé image/PDF link). The sanitizers accept it because the scheme is https. The same value can also be written straight through PostgREST on the student's own row, skipping even the scheme check. (2) Each time another student opens `/profile/<handle>` on desktop or phone, their browser requests the image (or pdf.js fetches the PDF); the attacker's server log records viewer IP, user-agent and timestamp. (3) Hand out per-recipient profile links (`?ref=a`, `?ref=b`), or rotate the logo URL per target, to resolve individual viewers. In the phone's `url(${logoUrl})` style a URL containing `)` can stack a second `url()` layer, but still only images load.

**Impact:** Deanonymised profile views: visitor IP, device fingerprint and view time leak to the profile owner and to whichever third-party host they chose. It silently contradicts the Privacy Policy's account of what a profile view reveals and the product's "counts public, identities private" rule. No script runs, no data in Vibe's database is exposed. Same harm class the S51 pass judged serious enough to add a host lock plus a DB CHECK for covers.

**What to do:** Apply the avatar/banner pin to `work_experience[].logo_url` and `current_on[].logoUrl`: accept only `isSupabaseHttpsUrl` + the student's own folder (both fields already have an upload path, profile-upload kind `logo`); coerce anything else to `null` on read as well as on write so existing rows are cleaned without a data migration. For external résumé links, show visitors a plain anchor (no `<img>`, no pdf.js fetch) or snapshot the file into the private `resumes` bucket at save time and serve it through `/api/resume`. Back it with a trigger modelled on `orgs_check_asset_refs` over the two jsonb columns so a direct PostgREST write cannot skip the check. Add a Content-Security-Policy with `img-src 'self' <supabase host> data: blob:` as the last line of defence. Update the Privacy Policy the same day if any external-link behaviour is kept.

**Where:** `src/lib/profile/work-experience.ts:25-50`, `src/lib/profile/current-on.ts:20-34`, `src/lib/profile/resume-doc-url.ts:13-19`, `src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:305-327`, `public/html/profile.html:10325-10329, 10347-10350, 5887-5896, 5938-5943, 5100-5111`, `src/components/mobile/ProfileMobile.tsx:5160`, `src/components/mobile/ResumeViewerMobile.tsx:799-800`. Reference implementation to copy: `src/app/api/me/profile/route.ts:84-96` (`parseMediaUrlField` + `isSupabaseHttpsUrl` + `ownProfilePhotoPath`) and DB trigger `orgs_check_asset_refs`.

### [LOW] A club officer could slip a fake link or a fake second event into students' personal calendars — likely, not yet tested against real calendar apps

**In plain terms:** When a student taps "Add to calendar" on a club event, Vibe hands their phone a small calendar file. An officer of a verified club could write an event description containing hidden line breaks that some calendar apps read as separate instructions, so the saved calendar entry carries a clickable link the officer chose, or a second made-up event ("Mandatory dues payment") that looks like it came from the club. Students trust what lands in their own calendar under a club's name, which makes this a believable way to phish for logins. A smaller side effect: a club whose name contains a colon or quotation mark produces a calendar file some apps refuse to import, so "Add to calendar" simply fails for that club. The reach is limited: only an officer of a real, verified club can write event text, and the common calendar apps on phones most likely just show an odd-looking description.

**What it is:** Both ICS builders serialise event title, description, location and the club name into RFC 5545 content lines with `escIcsText`, which escapes `\`, `;`, `,` and `\r?\n` only (`src/app/api/events/[id]/ics/route.ts:127-134`; same helper in `src/app/api/me/calendar/ics/route.ts:167-173`). A lone CR (U+000D not followed by LF), NEL (U+0085), VT/FF (U+000B/U+000C), U+001C-U+001E and U+2028/U+2029 pass through untouched. Event text validation is `asTrimmed()` + length caps + the word filter (`src/lib/events/validate.ts:63-64, 96-110`), with no control-character normalisation, and the DB CHECKs are length-only (`supabase/migrations/20260928110000_club_events_rules.sql:478-483`). JSON request bodies carry all of those characters (`\u000d`, `\u2028`). Importers that split with line-ending-agnostic routines (Python `str.splitlines()`, used by the `icalendar` library) treat each as a new content line. Separately, the club name goes into a parameter value unquoted (`ORGANIZER;CN=${escIcsText(e.organizer)}:mailto:…`, `ics/route.ts:110`): a `:` in the name terminates the parameter list early, and `\;` / `\,` backslash escapes are not valid inside a param value, so `Sigma: Rush` yields a malformed ORGANIZER line. Writers are limited to verified-club owners/admins (live grants: `authenticated` has no INSERT/UPDATE on `public.events`; club name is set only through the orgs API with a 2-50 char check at `src/app/api/orgs/route.ts:763`).

**How an attacker uses it:** (1) A verified-club officer calls `POST /api/events` with `"description": "Rush week details\u000dURL:https://evil.example/login\u000dATTACH:https://evil.example/agenda.pdf\u000dX-ALT-DESC;FMTTYPE=text/html:<a href=\"https://evil.example\">Sign in to RSVP</a>"`, or `\u000dEND:VEVENT\u000dBEGIN:VEVENT\u000dUID:x@vibe\u000dDTSTART:20261101T000000Z\u000dSUMMARY:Mandatory dues payment…` to inject a second VEVENT. `checkEventFields` accepts it (under 2000 chars, no banned words). (2) Students who RSVP tap "Add to calendar", downloading `/api/events/<id>/ics` or the bundled `/api/me/calendar/ics`; the file contains `DESCRIPTION:Rush week details<CR>URL:https://evil.example/login<CR>…` on what the server treats as one line. (3) A line-ending-agnostic importer parses `URL:`, `ATTACH:`, `X-ALT-DESC` or the extra `VEVENT` as genuine properties.

**Impact:** Calendar-entry phishing and spam against RSVP'd students under a club's trusted name (attacker-chosen URL, attachment, HTML description or fabricated extra events); malformed ORGANIZER lines break import for clubs whose name contains `:` or `"`. Bounded: writer must already be a verified-club officer, reader must be signed in, and clients that split strictly on CRLF see only an odd description. No data exposure, no server-side effect. Not yet confirmed with a live download into Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Python `icalendar`.

**What to do:** In `escIcsText`, normalise every line-break-class character before escaping: `.replace(/\r\n|\r|\n|\u0085|\u000b|\u000c|\u001c|\u001d|\u001e|\u2028|\u2029/g, "\\n")`, then strip remaining C0/C1 controls with `/[\x00-\x08\x0e-\x1f\x7f-\x9f]/g`. Emit the organizer as a quoted param with forbidden characters removed: `ORGANIZER;CN="${name.replace(/["\r\n;:,\\]/g, " ")}":mailto:…`. Add the same control-character strip to `checkEventFields` and the club-name check so stored text is clean at the source, and fold lines over 75 octets per RFC 5545 §3.1 so long descriptions import reliably. Verify with a live download into Apple Calendar, a Google Calendar import, and Python `icalendar`.

**Where:** `src/app/api/events/[id]/ics/route.ts:107-110, 127-134`; `src/app/api/me/calendar/ics/route.ts:147-151, 167-173`; `src/lib/events/validate.ts:63-64, 96-110`; `supabase/migrations/20260928110000_club_events_rules.sql:478-483`; `src/app/api/orgs/route.ts:763`.

## Data Privacy & Exposure

This section is about who can look at what a student put on their profile, and whether the people a student chose to shut out can still see it. The good news first: the pieces that matter most are behind the right doors. Resumes, the "what I'm here for" answers, and the "who viewed your profile" list cannot be pulled out by the wrong person, and the three findings below do not change that. What remains is one gap in friction (anyone can copy the whole campus directory quickly and quietly), one rule that Vibe enforces on its screens but the database underneath does not (blocking), and one small set of facts about every account that any signed-in student can pull in bulk. All three line up with, or need a same-day edit to, the Privacy Policy (the public page that tells students exactly what Vibe shows and to whom).

### [MEDIUM] Anyone on the internet can copy the entire student directory in minutes — confirmed on the live site today

**In plain terms:** Someone with no Vibe account can copy the public profile of every student on Vibe (name, photo, major, year, campus, the location line, website, work history, every post and every full-size picture) for the whole campus in a few minutes, and nothing slows them down or notices. A student who blocked someone is included, because blocks only work for people who are signed in. The result is a list of IU and Purdue students that lives outside Vibe forever, which is what a data broker buys and what a harasser who got blocked wants; if it happened in month one, it would be the first story anyone wrote about Vibe. Vibe already tells students these things are public, so the fix is not to hide them, it is to make copying them at scale slow, expensive and visible.

**What it is:** Three profile-shaped reads answer without a session and read with the service role (the server's all-access database login): `GET /api/users/[handle]/bootstrap`, `GET /api/users/[handle]/posts?limit=500`, `GET /api/orgs/[slug]/profile`, plus `GET /api/posts/[id]/media` which 307-redirects a signed-out caller to the full-resolution R2 object ("deliberately no signed-out gate"). None of the four calls `rateLimit()`; `src/proxy.ts` applies no limiter or bot check to anonymous `/api/*` GETs; `vercel.json` holds only crons, no firewall rules. Confirmed live 2026-10-08: each returns a JSON 404 for an unknown handle, never 401, and sends no `x-ratelimit-*` or `retry-after` headers. `bootstrap` returns the target's UUID, name, handle, school, school_verified, year, major, department, tagline, headline, location_text, website, interests, avatar/banner, campus, follower/following/connection counts, pinnedPostId and (unless hidden) bio, skills, work_experience and current_on; resume keys and looking_for are correctly stripped for anonymous callers. `/posts` accepts `limit` up to `MAX_LIMIT = 500`. `/api/orgs/[slug]/profile` hands an anonymous caller the 12 newest posts of every club, private clubs included, each with author id, handle, name and avatar. The 200-vs-404 split is a free handle oracle.

**How an attacker uses it:** Signed out, from any IP: (1) `GET /api/orgs/<slug>/profile` for each club slug (from Discover, share links, or a short dictionary) to collect author handles. (2) For each handle, `GET /api/users/<handle>/bootstrap` for the full profile including UUID, major, year, campus, location line, website, work history. (3) `GET /api/users/<handle>/posts?limit=500` for every personal post and its `/api/posts/<id>/media` URLs. (4) Fetch each media URL, follow the 307 to the signed R2 URL, download full-res. (5) Loop at hundreds of requests per minute; nothing answers 429. A first-name/last-name dictionary against step 2 enumerates the rest of the user base. At launch scale the whole campus takes minutes.

**Impact:** An off-platform dataset of IU/Purdue students (name, photo, major, year, campus, location line, website, employer history, all posts and media) assembled by a scraper, a data broker, or a harasser the student has blocked (Privacy Policy 5a says blocks do not apply signed out). Resume keys and "here for" answers stay withheld, so the most sensitive PII is out. It is also an unmetered cost surface: each bootstrap is 4–6 service-role queries plus a restriction read, paid by Vibe per request.

**What to do:** Add a per-network read limiter (`clientNetworkKey`, fail-open like the existing read limiters) to the three anonymous routes and to `/api/posts/[id]/media`: roughly 120/min per /24 (IPv4) or /64 (IPv6) on `bootstrap` and org profile, 60/min on `/posts`; cap the anonymous `limit` on `/posts` at 24 (the grid's first page). Turn on Vercel Firewall bot protection or an Attack Challenge rule for signed-out requests to `/api/users/*` and `/api/orgs/*/profile`. Keep unknown and restricted handles returning the same 404 shape and timing (already true); the limiter is what makes the handle oracle cost something. Narrowing the signed-out payload (department, location_text, website, work_experience) is a product call that requires the Privacy Policy 5a bullet to change the same day; the limiter needs no policy change.

**Where:** `src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:34-46` (column list), `:100-122` (no auth, service client), `:247-263` (anonymous strip = resume + looking_for only), no `rateLimit` import; `src/app/api/users/[handle]/posts/route.ts:11-12` (`MAX_LIMIT 500`), `:45-49`, `:73-81`; `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/profile/route.ts:61-77`, `:178-184`; `src/app/api/posts/[id]/media/route.ts:26-31`; `src/proxy.ts` (no limiter on anonymous GET `/api/*`); `vercel.json` (crons only); `src/app/legal/privacy/sections-4-5-a.tsx:10-34`.

### [LOW] A student who was blocked can still read the blocker's profile card from the database — confirmed in the live database, nobody affected yet

**In plain terms:** If a student blocks someone, that person can still pull up the blocker's current profile card (name, photo, campus, the location line, website, pinned post) by asking Vibe's database directly instead of going through Vibe's screens, and from the mismatch between the two answers they can work out that they were blocked. Nobody is affected today: there are 21 accounts and zero blocks. But blocks exist for exactly the people who would bother to do this, and the Privacy Policy promises a blocked person is only told "this account has restricted them", which is more than the database currently delivers.

**What it is:** Every app route hides a blocker from the person they blocked: `users/search`, `/api/search`, `/api/users/by-id` → 404, `bootstrap` → "restricted you" card with no fields, follow lists → empty, `posts_select_authenticated` → `NOT is_blocked_either_way`. The database row itself is not hidden. Live `pg_policies`: `users_select_authenticated` = `((id = (select auth.uid())) OR user_visible(id))` with no block clause, and `pg_attribute.attacl` grants `authenticated` SELECT on 22 `public.users` columns: id, name, handle, school, school_verified, year, major, department, avatar_url, banner_url, interests, created_at, tagline, website, headline, location_text, banner_gradient, handle_changed_at, pinned_post_id, profile_view_count, work_order_manual, school_system, campus_id. Migration `20260928120000_blocks_in_rls.sql:57-62` records this as deliberate ("a block clause there would null names in every users embed") and says hiding is done in the routes. The routes are not the boundary when the publishable key (the browser-side database login) ships in the bundle and PostgREST (Supabase's direct database-over-the-web door) is reachable with any session JWT (the signed-in session's bearer token).

**How an attacker uses it:** (1) Student B is blocked by student A. (2) B copies their own session access token from the Supabase auth cookie or localStorage in any signed-in tab, and the publishable key from the bundle. (3) B runs `GET https://<project-ref>.supabase.co/rest/v1/users?handle=eq.<A's handle>&select=name,handle,avatar_url,school,major,year,campus_id,school_system,location_text,website,tagline,headline,interests,pinned_post_id,profile_view_count,handle_changed_at` with `apikey: <publishable>` and `Authorization: Bearer <B's JWT>`. (4) 200 with A's current row, while `/api/users/by-id/<A>` answers 404 "User not found" to the same session, which confirms the block. (5) B polls daily for A's new photo, campus, location line, website, pinned post and view count; `pinned_post_id` then feeds the anonymous `/api/posts/<id>/media` redirect for the full-resolution image.

**Impact:** A block is cosmetic at the data layer for the profile card: a harasser keeps live access to the blocker's name, photo, campus, location line and website, and learns they were blocked from the API/REST mismatch. This contradicts the Blocks paragraph of Privacy Policy 5a, and the standing rule is that any change to what the app shows needs a same-day policy change. Safety-relevant for the stalking cases blocks exist for. Live scale today is 21 users and 0 block rows, so no one is affected yet; the gap is structural.

**What to do:** Pick one and update Privacy Policy 5a the same day. (a) Add `AND NOT is_blocked_either_way((select auth.uid()), id)` to `users_select_authenticated` and move the few embeds the migration worried about (the Blocked-users list you unblock from, group-chat sender names, the thread roster) to service-role reads scoped by the route, the way `bootstrap` and followers already do. (b) If the embed cost is unacceptable, narrow the direct path instead: `REVOKE SELECT (location_text, website, headline, tagline, interests, pinned_post_id, profile_view_count, created_at, handle_changed_at, department, banner_url, banner_gradient) ON public.users FROM authenticated`, so PostgREST yields only the card columns `hydrateUserCards`/search/notifications actually embed (id, name, handle, avatar_url, major, year, school, school_verified, campus_id, school_system), then state in 5a that a blocked person may still see your name, photo, major and campus where you appear in shared spaces. Either way, add a drift test that fails if `users_select_authenticated` or the `users` column ACL changes without a policy-text review.

**Where:** `pg_policies` → `users.users_select_authenticated` qual `((id = ( SELECT auth.uid() AS uid)) OR user_visible(id))` (live); `pg_attribute.attacl` on `public.users`: 22 columns `authenticated=r` (live); `supabase/migrations/20260928120000_blocks_in_rls.sql:57-62`; `src/app/api/users/by-id/[id]/route.ts:17-23,58`; `src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:203-245`; `src/app/legal/privacy/sections-4-5-a.tsx:54-59`.

### [LOW] Any signed-in student can pull every account's total profile views, creation date and last handle change in one go — confirmed in the live database; already disclosed

**In plain terms:** Any signed-in student can ask the database for three facts about every other account at once, facts no Vibe screen shows about other people: how many times their profile has been viewed in total, when the account was made, and when they last changed their handle. Vibe's privacy page already says a signed-in account could read these, so nothing is being hidden from students. The concern is that it lets someone rank the whole campus by popularity, or spot who just joined or just renamed themselves, with no screen, no friction and no record of it happening. It is cheap to close, and closing it lets the privacy page promise less rather than more.

*(The input record for this finding was cut off after its title. The detail below is limited to what this report could confirm directly in the code and the live database; the severity is inferred from that evidence and from the fact that the exposure is already disclosed.)*

**What it is:** The `public.users` column ACL (the per-column read permission list) grants `authenticated` SELECT on `profile_view_count`, `created_at` and `handle_changed_at` (live `pg_attribute.attacl`, part of the 22-column list in the previous finding). No app screen shows these for other people: `bootstrap`'s `PUBLIC_PROFILE_SELECT` (`src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:34-46`) omits all three, and the only reader of `profile_view_count` in `src/` is `GET /api/me/profile-views` (`route.ts:186-187`), which reads the caller's own row for the all-time tile. The direct PostgREST path, under `users_select_authenticated` (`user_visible(id)`), is open to every signed-in session. Privacy Policy 5b (`src/app/legal/privacy/sections-4-5-a.tsx:80-85`) discloses exactly this: "For any account: when it was made, when its handle last changed and its total profile views."

**How an attacker uses it:** With the publishable key and any session JWT: `GET https://<project-ref>.supabase.co/rest/v1/users?select=id,handle,name,profile_view_count,created_at,handle_changed_at&order=profile_view_count.desc` with `Range` paging. One request per 1,000 rows yields a campus-wide popularity ranking, a "joined this week" list, and a "renamed recently" list (useful to re-find someone who changed their handle to shake a follower). Nothing logs the read; `/api/me/profile-views` is never touched.

**Impact:** Low today. A derived leaderboard of the whole campus by profile views, and a way to track account creation and handle changes that the product deliberately does not surface about others (`campus_set_at` is already withheld for the same reason). Because 5b discloses it, there is no policy breach; the cost is that the policy has to admit a read no screen offers, and that the "no vanity metrics" principle is only enforced by the UI.

**What to do:** `REVOKE SELECT (profile_view_count, created_at, handle_changed_at) ON public.users FROM authenticated`. Unlike the `profile_views` table (migration `20260928140000`, where a column revoke was a no-op because the grant was table-level), `users` already carries column-level ACLs, so this revoke takes effect; pre-flight and post-check `pg_attribute.attacl` in the same transaction and `NOTIFY pgrst, 'reload schema'`. Before shipping, confirm the self-reads keep working: `src/app/settings/page.tsx:45` and `src/app/api/me/profile-bootstrap/route.ts:25` select `handle_changed_at`/`created_at` alongside `email` (not in the authenticated grant), so they should already run on the service role; `src/app/api/me/handle/route.ts` and `onboarding-complete/route.ts:33` read `handle_changed_at` for the cooldown and must stay on the service role or a SECURITY DEFINER function. Then shorten the 5b bullet the same day to drop "when it was made, when its handle last changed and its total profile views". Fold this into option (b) of the previous finding if that path is chosen, and cover the three columns in the same drift test.

**Where:** `pg_attribute.attacl` on `public.users` (live, `authenticated=r` on `profile_view_count`, `created_at`, `handle_changed_at`); `pg_policies` → `users_select_authenticated`; `src/app/api/me/profile-views/route.ts:186-187,233`; `src/app/api/users/[handle]/bootstrap/route.ts:34-46`; `src/app/settings/page.tsx:45`; `src/app/api/me/profile-bootstrap/route.ts:25`; `src/app/api/me/handle/route.ts:14,63`; `src/app/api/me/onboarding-complete/route.ts:33,181`; `src/app/legal/privacy/sections-4-5-a.tsx:80-85`; `supabase/migrations/20260928140000_profile_views_server_only.sql` (the column-vs-table revoke note).

## Abuse, Rate-Limiting & Cost / Monetary Risk

This section is about the ways one person can run up Vibe's bills or jam the parts of the app that everyone shares (email, file storage, the database), and about what happens to the files students upload. The basics are in place: sign-up and the waitlist have hard caps, every upload has a size limit, and the password-reset form limits each email address. But four gaps remain, and each one can be worked by a single free account or, in one case, by someone with no account at all. None of them leaks anyone's private data; the harm is stalled sign-ups, a slower app for everyone, a storage bill that never goes down, and one way to make a Vibe link open a stranger's web page.

### [Medium] Anyone can use the "forgot password" form to use up Vibe's daily email allowance  — confirmed in the current code

**In plain terms:** Someone who knows two or three Vibe users' school emails can keep clicking "forgot password" on them, with no account of their own, until Vibe has sent its whole day's worth of email. From that moment, every new student trying to sign up waits for a verification code that never arrives, waitlist invites stop, and the two or three people whose addresses were used get flooded with reset emails they did not ask for. During the tester wave this is the difference between "I got in" and "Vibe is broken," and it costs the attacker nothing.

**What it is:** `POST /api/auth/password-reset` is the only unauthenticated path that triggers `admin.generateLink` plus `resend.emails.send`. It is throttled only per IP (5 per 15 min) and per address (3 per hour, hashed). There is no global or daily email budget, unlike the waitlist, which uses `waitlist-global` (300/h, failClosed) plus a 30/day confirm cap and a 10/network/day cap. Two weaknesses compound this: (1) the IP limiter key is `pw-reset-ip:${clientIp(req)}` (`src/lib/rate-limit.ts:35`), the raw address, not `clientNetworkKey`/`networkBucket` (the IPv6 /64 bucket every other network limiter uses), so an IPv6 host gets a fresh 5/15min allowance per address it picks; (2) the limiter is fail-open (no `failClosed`), so any DB slowdown removes the cap entirely. Resend usage today is near zero (`email_sends`: 1 row in 30 days) and the Resend upgrade is still owed, so the realistic ceiling is the free tier (~100/day, 3,000/month). That one quota also carries .edu verification codes, personal-email codes, the yearly school check-in codes, and waitlist invites.

**How an attacker uses it:** Collect a few real addresses (auth now uses the .edu address per S75; campus directories expose `firstname.lastname@iu.edu`). Submit the reset form for each at 3/hour. Every address with an account produces a real `generateLink` + Resend send. Two known accounts at 3/hour is ~144 sends/day, over the ~100/day free-tier ceiling. With IPv6 rotation the per-IP cap never triggers. The constant-200 body and `after()` deferral mean no enumeration, so this is purely cost/denial.

**Impact:** The shared transactional-email channel stops: no .edu code, no sign-up completion, no waitlist invites, plus mail-bombing of the targeted users. On the free tier the cost is a frozen onboarding day rather than a bill; on a paid plan with overage it becomes money. Realistic worst case today: a full day of blocked sign-ups per day the attacker bothers, for the price of a script.

**What to do:** Add a global hourly and daily email budget shared across every sender (mirror `waitlist-global` + the daily cap, `failClosed: true`) so total Resend spend is bounded regardless of how many addresses are targeted. Key the per-IP limiter on `clientNetworkKey(req)` instead of raw `clientIp(req)`. Lower the per-address allowance to roughly 3/day. In the Resend dashboard, set the account's own daily sending cap below the plan limit as a backstop.

**Where:** `src/app/api/auth/password-reset/route.ts`, `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:35` (`pw-reset-ip` key), `src/lib/resend*.ts`; compare with the waitlist limiter keys `waitlist-global` and the per-day confirm cap.

### [Medium] Dozens of app actions have no speed limit, so one account can slow Vibe for everyone and switch off the other safety caps  — confirmed in the current code

**In plain terms:** One ordinary account can send the same tiny request to Vibe thousands of times a minute (the "I'm still here" ping, "I viewed this post," "I viewed this profile," reposting) and nothing tells it to stop. Each one makes the database do real work, so the app gets sluggish for every student at once, and the hosting bill climbs with it. Worse, when the database is struggling, the email and upload limits described elsewhere in this section are built to give up rather than block, so a flood here quietly disables those protections too.

**What it is:** 37 mutating handlers (POST/PATCH/PUT/DELETE) call no `rateLimit` at all. The hot ones: `/api/me/heartbeat` issues an UPDATE on `public.users` on every call; `/api/posts/[id]/view` and `/api/profile-views` run `postAccessForCaller` reads plus a SECURITY DEFINER RPC per call; `/api/posts/[id]/repost` POST runs `requireCanPublish` + `postAccessForCaller` + an author block-check (3+ reads) per call. Also unthrottled: `/api/me/threads/[id]/typing`, `/api/me/threads/[id]/messages/[messageId]/react`, `/api/me/otto/reminders`, `/api/me/personal-events`, `/api/me/block`, `/api/me/mute`, `/api/me/dismiss-suggestion`, `/api/me/notifications/mark-read`, and most thread sub-routes. The engagement RPCs dedupe counter writes per (id, user, day), so count inflation from one account is bounded to +1/day, but the number of calls, and therefore the read/RPC/UPDATE work, is not. Because the DB-backed cost limiters elsewhere are fail-open, a Postgres slowdown caused here removes the email and upload caps at the same time.

**How an attacker uses it:** From one authenticated session, loop any of these as fast as the network allows. Hammering `/api/me/heartbeat` produces a continuous stream of UPDATEs on the most-read table; hammering `/api/posts/[id]/view` or `/api/profile-views` across many ids forces unbounded access-check reads plus RPC executions. None of them ever returns 429.

**Impact:** Supabase compute saturation, connection-pool pressure, WAL and row churn on `public.users`, and per-request egress, from one free account. The bill is mostly bounded by the Supabase plan tier, so the realistic worst case is the app becoming slow or briefly unavailable for everyone during the flood, pressure to buy a larger compute tier (tens of dollars a month), and, as a side effect, every fail-open cost limiter switching off while it lasts. This is the force-multiplier for the two findings on either side of it.

**What to do:** Wire the existing per-user `rateLimit` into these handlers: heartbeat at about 1 per 15–30 s, view and profile-view generous but present (for example 60/min), repost tight (for example 10/min). Add one cheap shared per-user write limiter across the whole `/api/me/*` surface to cover the long tail. Reconsider making the DB rate limiter fail-closed for a short window after an RPC error, and add a Vercel Firewall rate-limit rule as an edge-level cap so a DB slowdown cannot disable every cost control at once.

**Where:** `src/app/api/me/heartbeat/route.ts`, `src/app/api/posts/[id]/view/route.ts`, `src/app/api/profile-views/route.ts`, `src/app/api/posts/[id]/repost/route.ts`, `src/app/api/me/threads/[id]/**`, `src/app/api/me/{block,mute,dismiss-suggestion,personal-events,otto/reminders,notifications/mark-read}/route.ts`; limiter in `src/lib/rate-limit.ts`.

### [Medium] A student can upload a web page disguised as a photo, and a Vibe media link will open it  — confirmed in the current code

**In plain terms:** When someone uploads a picture or video, Vibe checks the file type they claim, but the storage service never checks what was actually sent. So a person can upload a fake sign-in page labelled as a photo, and anyone who opens that "photo" from a post, a club banner, or a group chat lands on that page instead. The page cannot read the viewer's Vibe login, but it looks like it came from Vibe, which is exactly what a scammer wants when asking a student to "sign in again" or enter a card number. One such incident is the kind of story that spreads across a campus in a day.

**What it is:** Every presigned PUT signer in `src/lib/r2.ts` sets `ContentType` on the `PutObjectCommand`, but `@aws-sdk/s3-request-presigner` treats `content-type` as unsignable; the file's own comment says so ("a default for the stored object, not an enforced constraint"). Only `Content-Length` is bound into the signature (`assertPutContentLength`). The upload-url routes validate `body.contentType` against an allowlist (`ALLOWED_TYPES`, `IMAGE_EXTS`/`VIDEO_EXTS`, `IMAGE_TYPES`/`VIDEO_TYPES`) and pick the key extension from it, but the browser (or curl) can send any `Content-Type` on the PUT and R2 stores that one. On the serving side, `GET /api/posts/[id]/media`, `GET /api/orgs/[slug]/asset/[kind]` and `GET /api/me/threads/[id]/messages/[messageId]/media` all 307-redirect to a signed `GetObjectCommand` URL with no `ResponseContentType` or `ResponseContentDisposition` set, so R2 serves the uploader's type. The `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` rule in `next.config.ts:89` applies to connectvibe.app responses only; the final response comes from the R2 host, which sends no nosniff and no CSP (a browser rule listing which code may run on a page). No MIME sniff or magic-byte check happens at publish/send time.

**How an attacker uses it:** Request an upload URL for `image/jpeg` with the correct `sizeBytes`, then PUT an HTML file to it with `Content-Type: text/html`. Publish the post (or attach it in a thread, or set it as a club banner via `/api/orgs/[slug]/upload-url`). Anyone who opens the media link is redirected to the R2 URL, which renders as a web page. Because the object lives on the R2 origin, not connectvibe.app, the script cannot read Vibe cookies or storage; the realistic payload is a convincing phishing page, a malicious download prompt, or a redirect to a scam site, reached through a connectvibe.app link.

**Impact:** Stored content that renders as attacker-controlled HTML from a link that starts on Vibe's domain. Trust and safety harm (phishing, scam pages, offensive pages) rather than account takeover; also a plausible App Store review complaint if a reviewer hits one.

**What to do:** (1) Bind the type into the PUT signature: pass `signableHeaders: new Set(["content-type"])` (and `unhoistableHeaders` so it stays a header, not a query param) to `getSignedUrl` in every PUT signer, and have the client send exactly that `Content-Type`, so a mismatched PUT is rejected with 403. (2) On every GET signer, set `ResponseContentType` to the type the app recorded (from the key extension or the DB row) and `ResponseContentDisposition: inline; filename="..."`, which are signed query parameters R2 honours, so the served type is app-chosen regardless of what was stored. (3) At publish/send/banner-save time, `HeadObject` the key and reject if `ContentType` is outside the allowlist; optionally sniff the first bytes. (4) If media ever moves to a custom R2 domain, add `nosniff` and a restrictive CSP there via a Cloudflare transform rule.

**Where:** `src/lib/r2.ts` (every `signClipPutUrl`/`sign*PutUrl` and `sign*GetUrl`), `src/app/api/me/clip-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/me/messages-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/me/group-photo-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/posts/[id]/media/route.ts:119-144`, `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/asset/[kind]/route.ts:76-80`, `src/app/api/me/threads/[id]/messages/[messageId]/media/route.ts:91`, `next.config.ts:89`.

### [Medium] One verified account can fill Vibe's file storage with videos nobody ever posts, and the bill for them never goes away  — confirmed in the current code

**In plain terms:** Vibe lets a verified student ask for an upload slot up to 20 times every 10 minutes, and each slot can take a 200 MB video. If they upload the video but never actually post it, the file sits in Vibe's storage forever, because nothing ever goes back to clean up files that were uploaded and then abandoned. One person doing this all day adds roughly half a terabyte, and Vibe pays rent on that space every month from then on with no way to get it back short of someone hand-deleting files.

**What it is:** `POST /api/me/clip-upload-url`, `/api/me/messages-upload-url`, `/api/me/group-photo-upload-url` and `/api/orgs/[slug]/upload-url` each cap minting at 20 per 10 minutes per user and bind `Content-Length` into the signature (`assertPutContentLength` in `src/lib/r2.ts`), so a single PUT cannot exceed its declared size. But the maxima are large (clip 200 MB, message video 200 MB, message image 15 MB, banner 6 MB) and there is no per-user daily or total byte ceiling. A signed PUT writes the object immediately; the DB row that references it is created only by a later publish/send call. Deletion sweeps exist (`deleteR2Prefix`, `deleteR2FolderExcept`) but only on account/club deletion; a grep of `src/lib` and migrations finds no GC of unreferenced `clips/<uid>/…` or `messages/<channelId>/…` keys, only a count-only script (`delete-media-store.ts`). Orphans persist forever. `clip-upload-url` and the org route require a verified/publishable account, which limits who can do this, but any legitimate tester qualifies.

**How an attacker uses it:** Mint 20 clip-upload URLs per 10-minute window, PUT a 200 MB object to each, never call publish-post. That is ~4 GB per 10 minutes, ~24 GB/hour, ~576 GB/day of objects no DB row references and no sweep removes. Repeat across windows indefinitely; the videos never count against any post limit because they are never published.

**Impact:** Unbounded Cloudflare R2 storage growth from one free (verified) account, with no reclaim mechanism. At R2's ~$0.015/GB-month, one day of this is roughly $8–9 per month forever, one week roughly $60 per month forever, plus Class A write operations (negligible at this rate). Slow but real and permanent cost, and the 10 GB free tier is gone within the first half hour.

**What to do:** Add a per-user daily byte and object budget for issued upload URLs (not just 20/10min), keyed through `rateLimit`. Implement an orphan sweep: a scheduled job that lists `clips/<uid>/…` and `messages/<channelId>/…` keys older than 24–48 h, checks for a matching DB row, and deletes the rest; or upload under an `incoming/` prefix with an R2 lifecycle rule that expires objects after 48 h unless the publish/send call copies them to their permanent key. Check the Cloudflare dashboard for an existing lifecycle rule on the bucket; none is visible from the repo.

**Where:** `src/app/api/me/clip-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/me/messages-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/me/group-photo-upload-url/route.ts`, `src/app/api/orgs/[slug]/upload-url/route.ts`, `src/lib/r2.ts` (`assertPutContentLength`, `deleteR2Prefix`, `deleteR2FolderExcept`), `scripts/delete-media-store.ts`, R2 bucket lifecycle settings (Cloudflare dashboard).

## Legal & Compliance

This is about the promises Vibe makes to students in writing and whether the app actually keeps them: what Vibe says it keeps, what it deletes, who the company is, and what happens when something goes wrong between two students. The privacy page went live on Oct 5 and mostly matches what the code does. Two gaps remain, and both land on the people least able to absorb them: a student who was sent something abusive and reported it, and the company itself in any dispute.

### [HIGH] A reported photo or video in a chat can be wiped for good when the sender deletes their account — confirmed in today's code

**In plain terms:** A photo or video that a student reported as an intimate image or child abuse material is erased from Vibe permanently the moment the person who sent it deletes their own account, and the same wipe runs on its own, with nobody watching, when someone ignores the yearly school check-in long enough. The victim is left with a report that points at nothing, and Vibe cannot hand the material to the national child-safety center or the police, even though federal law requires keeping it for 90 days after a child-abuse report and Vibe's own child-safety page promises "we keep the records the law requires." That is a safety failure for the student who was hurt, a broken public promise, and a legal exposure for the company.

**What it is:** The account-deletion purge in `src/lib/profile/storage-purge.ts` builds an "evidence to keep" set only for posts (moderator-removed posts and posts with an open or actioned report) and for a profile photo while a user report is open. `loadEvidence()` queries `reports` with `.eq("target_type","post")` (line 300) and `.eq("target_type","user")` (line 326) only; reports with `target_type = 'message'` are never read. Line 375 then runs `deleteR2Keys(await listUserMessageMediaKeys(uid))` with no exclusion set, so every object under `messages/<channelId>/<file>` in R2 (Cloudflare's file storage) that the leaving user's own messages reference is deleted. `purgeableMessageMediaKeys()` in `src/lib/posts/media-ownership.ts:128-150` filters only on channel folder and `sharedWithOthers`. Moderator removal does not help: `POST /api/admin/content/remove` quarantines media only when `type === "post"` (`route.ts:198`); a removed message's file stays where it is and is still purgeable. The report row survives, but `MESSAGE_SNAPSHOT_COLUMNS` (`src/app/api/me/reports/route.ts:65`) stores `media_url` as text, so after the purge it is a dangling pointer. `src/lib/auth/school-checkin-job.ts:149` calls the same `purgeUserUploads(userId)` and then `deleteUser` at line 156, unattended. Privacy section 9's evidence list (`sections-9-10.tsx`) names posts, profile images and logos only, so the policy is consistent with the code but contradicts the child-safety page (`child-safety/page.tsx:124`) and the statutory duty.

**How an attacker uses it:** 1. Any signed-in, verified student sends an intimate image or CSAM in a DM; the file lands at `messages/<channelId>/<uuid>.jpg` in R2. 2. The victim taps Report and picks "Intimate image shared without consent" or "Child sexual abuse or exploitation"; `POST /api/me/reports` stores `target_type='message'` with a snapshot `{media_url: 'messages/<channel>/<file>'}`. 3. A moderator removes the message via `POST /api/admin/content/remove {type:'message'}`; nothing is quarantined. 4. The sender calls `DELETE /api/me` with `{"confirm_handle":"<own handle>"}`. `loadEvidence()` ignores message reports, `listUserMessageMediaKeys()` returns the file, `deleteR2Keys()` removes it. 5. Vibe can no longer produce the material for NCMEC or law enforcement. Variant: the sender does nothing and lets the yearly check-in lapse (14 + 90 days); the cron runs the identical purge with no human in the loop.

**Impact:** Loss of the material needed to make or support a NCMEC CyberTipline report and any law-enforcement request. 18 U.S.C. 2258A requires a provider to preserve reported CSAM for 90 days; knowing failures to report carry fines up to $150,000 for a first violation. Vibe also cannot prove it handled a TAKE IT DOWN Act / intimate-image complaint, and the published Child Safety Standards and Privacy section 9 ("we keep the records the law requires") become statements the code does not back. For the victim, the one copy that proved what was sent is gone.

**What to do:** In `loadEvidence()`, also collect (a) `reports` with `target_type='message'` in status open or actioned and (b) the user's `messages` rows where `removed_at is not null`, and pass their `media_url` keys to `deleteR2Keys` as an exclusion set, mirroring what `evidenceToKeep` already does for clips. In `/api/admin/content/remove` for `type === 'message'`, record the R2 key as evidence (copy to an `evidence/` prefix or insert into a legal-hold table) so a later purge can see it. Add a per-account legal-hold flag that makes both `DELETE /api/me` and the check-in cron refuse the purge and return the policy's existing "unless a report or the law needs something kept" message. With the lawyer: write the 2258A/NCMEC runbook (who files, the 90-day hold, who holds R2 access) and update Privacy section 9 and the child-safety page to list chat media as evidence. The code fix is a few dozen lines; the reporting obligation needs the lawyer.

**Where:** `src/lib/profile/storage-purge.ts:271-336` (`loadEvidence`), `:375`; `src/lib/posts/media-ownership.ts:128-150`; `src/app/api/admin/content/remove/route.ts:197-198`; `src/app/api/me/route.ts` (DELETE); `src/app/api/me/reports/route.ts:65`; `src/lib/auth/school-checkin-job.ts:149-156`; `src/app/legal/child-safety/page.tsx:124`; `src/app/legal/privacy/sections-9-10.tsx` (section 9).

## Infrastructure, Config & Dependencies

This section is about the plumbing under Vibe rather than any one feature: the outside code the app is built from, the safety settings the site sends to every browser, and how sign-ins and company secrets are handled. The overall state is sound. Nothing here is a hole a stranger can walk through today. The three items are about staying ahead of trouble: one overdue upgrade, one missing safety net, and some dead weight that should come out before the Oct 16 store submission.

### [Medium] The framework Vibe runs on is four fixes behind, and one of the missing fixes is serious — not exploitable today, confirmed on the live site

**In plain terms:** The software Vibe is built on top of has published four small updates that Vibe has not taken. One of them closes a flaw that would let an attacker run their own code on Vibe's servers, the same servers that hold every student's account, messages and photos. Today Vibe does not use that part of the software in the way the flaw needs, so nobody can use it against the app right now. But the next time someone adds a link preview that shows a student's name or a club's name (a very normal feature to add), the door opens, and a stranger could read or change every account. The upgrade takes a few minutes and should happen before the store submission.

**What it is:** `package.json:31` pins `"next": "^16.3.4"` and the lockfile (last changed 2026-09-25) resolves 16.3.4, which is what the live build `dpl_5W4wxnWsM6TupJHMhaoj8mFKHWor` carries. npm has published 16.3.5–16.3.8 and 16.4.0. The 16.3.x patches close seven advisories: GHSA-vcvr-r3jv-pc5j (critical, remote code execution in the Node.js `next/og` `ImageResponse` when attacker-controlled strings reach SVG content, attributes or styles; fixed in 16.3.6), GHSA-cjq9-62q9-8jv4 (server-side request forgery in image optimisation; needs `images.remotePatterns`), GHSA-mcj8-r9mp-w47p and GHSA-4jqv-mc3x-m676 (SSG/ISR cache poisoning; need a root-level catch-all page or self-hosted Pages Router), GHSA-f87g-xv8r-7p7x (metadata image routes ignore `dynamicParams`; needs a dynamic segment with a metadata image inside it), GHSA-3w37-wq28-93x7 (Draft Mode leak via `use cache`), and GHSA-39w2-rjm5-chcv (the `next dev` MCP endpoint is readable by any website open in the same browser). Reachability in this app: the share card in `src/lib/og/share-card.tsx` IS rendered by the vulnerable Node `ImageResponse`, but lines 20–30 are all constants and lines 90–186 take no parameters and never read the request. `next.config.ts` has no `images` block. The only dynamic segments are `posts/[id]`, `orgs/[handle]`, `profile/[handle]`; none is a catch-all and none contains a metadata image file. `grep -rE 'draftMode|use cache|revalidate|cacheTag' src` returns nothing.

**How an attacker uses it:** Today, no request to www.connectvibe.app triggers any of the seven. The realistic path is the next feature: someone adds `/profile/[handle]/opengraph-image.tsx` (or the club equivalent) that puts the display name or bio into the JSX while still on 16.3.4 or 16.3.5. An attacker sets their display name to a crafted SVG-injection string, then gets their profile link unfurled by iMessage, Slack or X, or simply fetches `/profile/<handle>/opengraph-image`. The string executes inside the Vercel function that renders the image, which runs with `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, the R2 credentials and the Stripe secret in its environment. Secondary, dev-only: Franky runs `next dev`, opens an attacker's page in the same browser, and that page reads the repo path, route inventory and dev log lines from `localhost:3000` (not env values).

**Impact:** Currently none exploitable. Latent: full remote code execution in a production serverless function, which means read and write of every user record through the service-role key, plus R2 media and Stripe. The gap between "safe" and "critical" is one ordinary feature.

**What to do:** Before Oct 16: `npm install next@16.3.8 eslint-config-next@16.3.8` (or 16.4.0 after reading `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` for breaking notes), then `npm run typecheck && npm run build`, commit the lockfile, deploy, and confirm `/api/version` reports the new build. Add a one-line comment at the top of `src/lib/og/share-card.tsx`: "No request-time or user-supplied value may enter this JSX until Next ≥ 16.3.6 (GHSA-vcvr-r3jv-pc5j)". Until upgraded, keep `next dev` and untrusted browsing in separate browser profiles.

**Where:** `package.json:31`, `package-lock.json`, `src/lib/og/share-card.tsx`, `src/app/opengraph-image.tsx`, `src/app/twitter-image.tsx`, `next.config.ts`.

### [Medium] No browser rule limiting which code can run on Vibe's pages, and the sign-in proof is readable by page code — confirmed on the live site

**In plain terms:** Vibe's pages are missing a standard safety rule: a short note the site sends with every page telling the browser "only run code that came from Vibe." On top of that, the proof-of-login that keeps a student signed in is stored where any code running on the page can read it. Put together: if an attacker ever manages to slip their own code into a page, for example through a profile name or a message that gets shown without being cleaned up, that code can copy the student's sign-in and the attacker becomes that student, reading their messages, posting as them, and for the three founder accounts, running moderation. Nothing today is known to allow that slip. This is the net that would catch it if it happens, and right now there is no net.

**What it is:** The global header block in `next.config.ts` (`headers()`, lines 77–106) ships HSTS with preload, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` and a `Permissions-Policy`, but deliberately omits `Content-Security-Policy`; the comment at lines 80–82 says the `/html` prototypes rely on inline scripts, so a nonce rollout was deferred. A live GET of `https://www.connectvibe.app/` confirms every baseline header is present and no `content-security-policy` header exists. The session cookies are set by `@supabase/ssr` with its defaults (`node_modules/@supabase/ssr/dist/main/utils/constants.js:6-7`: `sameSite: "lax"`, `httpOnly: false`, no `Secure` attribute); `src/lib/supabase/server.ts`, `middleware.ts` and `browser.ts` forward the library options unchanged. So the `sb-...-auth-token` cookie is readable from `document.cookie`. The signed-in surfaces are the static shells under `public/html/**`, which render live `/api` data with heavy `innerHTML` (`profile.html` 96 sites, `messages.html` 31, `_postViewer.js` 28) and 7–21 inline `<script>` tags each — exactly the code shape CSP exists to backstop. `SameSite=lax` and HSTS already cover CSRF and HTTP downgrade; the residual gap is script-injection containment plus the cookie amplifier.

**How an attacker uses it:** Get attacker-controlled HTML rendered through one of the `innerHTML` sinks in `public/html` (a profile, club or message string surfaced by `_postViewer.js` or `profile.html`). With no CSP, an injected `<img onerror>` or `<script>` runs in the connectvibe.app origin, reads `document.cookie`, POSTs the auth cookie to an external collector, and the attacker replays it. No `connect-src` restriction exists to block the exfiltration call. Blast radius includes officers and the three platform admins (moderation, waitlist reveal).

**Impact:** Any single script-injection bug becomes full session takeover, with no header-level containment and no egress limit. The missing CSP also removes the last line of defense for the entire inline-script prototype surface that is the actual signed-in app.

**What to do:** Ship a CSP, starting in `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only` if needed: move the `/html` inline scripts into external files (most already live in `public/html/*.js`) or add per-response nonces, then set `script-src 'self' 'nonce-...'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self'; connect-src 'self' <supabase host> <r2 host> <sentry host>`. The `connect-src` clause alone blunts cookie exfiltration even before the nonce migration finishes. Separately, pass `cookieOptions: { secure: true }` to `createServerClient` / `createBrowserClient`. Note that `@supabase/ssr` sets `httpOnly: false` on purpose because its browser client reads the cookie; flipping it to `true` only works if every browser-side Supabase call is routed through `/api` instead of `browser.ts`, so audit `createBrowserClient` usage first. Until then, treat the cookie as JS-reachable in the threat model: audit every `innerHTML` sink in `public/html` for unescaped user data and add a sanitizer or Trusted Types.

**Where:** `next.config.ts:77-106`, `src/lib/supabase/server.ts`, `src/lib/supabase/middleware.ts`, `src/lib/supabase/browser.ts`, `public/html/profile.html`, `public/html/messages.html`, `public/html/_postViewer.js`.

### [Low] A build-time developer tool is installed as if it were part of the live app, bringing 278 unused packages with it — confirmed in the current lockfile

**In plain terms:** A tool Vibe only uses while a developer is building screens is listed as if the live app needs it. It drags in about 280 extra pieces of other people's code, 38 percent of everything installed, none of which Vibe's pages ever run. Two problems follow. Eleven of the fifteen warnings in the automated safety check are about these unused pieces, which buries the warnings that matter. And every one of those pieces gets installed on the build machine that holds Vibe's secrets, so a bad update to any of them would get a chance to run there. Dropping the tool from the live app's list is a small cleanup that shrinks that exposure and makes the warning list honest.

**What it is:** `package.json:37` lists `"shadcn": "^4.6.0"` under `dependencies`. `shadcn` is an interactive code generator; no file in `src/` imports it as JavaScript (`grep -rn "from 'shadcn'\|require('shadcn')" src` returns nothing; `components.json` only references its `$schema`). The one real use is `src/app/globals.css:3` `@import "shadcn/tailwind.css"`, which resolves to `node_modules/shadcn/dist/tailwind.css`, a 95-line file of accordion/collapsible keyframes and theme tokens. Because it sits in `dependencies`, `npm ci` on every Vercel build installs its full runtime tree: `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk` 1.29.0 (GHSA-6qxp-vccf-f47h, high), `express` 5.2.1 → `proxy-addr` 2.0.7 (GHSA-jqcg-44mw-7w3h, the audit's second "critical"), `hono` 4.13.5, `express-rate-limit` → `ip-address` 10.7.0, `msw` 2.13.6 (which has a `postinstall` script: `node -e "import('./config/scripts/postinstall.js')..."`), and `ts-morph` 26 → `fast-glob` / `micromatch` / `braces` / `brace-expansion`. `npm audit` today: 15 findings (2 critical, 10 high, 3 moderate); 11 are in this tree. The remaining four are `next` (the finding above), `eslint-config-next` / `@next/eslint-plugin-next` (dev tooling, same `fast-glob` chain), and `sharp` (librsvg advisory CVE-2026-96889), which deserves its own look once the noise is gone.

**How an attacker uses it:** Not at request time; none of this code is bundled or executed by the deployed app, so the express/hono/MCP advisories are unreachable by anyone sending requests to connectvibe.app. The exposure is supply-chain: a compromised release of any of these 278 packages (or of `msw`, whose install script already runs) executes during `npm ci` on Vercel, inside an environment that holds `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, the R2 credentials, the Stripe secret and the Resend key. The second cost is signal: the eleven inherited audit lines make it easy to stop reading the list and miss a real one.

**Impact:** Low today. Larger install surface on a machine holding every production secret, and an audit report that is mostly noise. Nothing a student sees.

**What to do:** Copy `node_modules/shadcn/dist/tailwind.css` into the repo (for example `src/app/shadcn-tailwind.css`), change `globals.css:3` to import that local file, then `npm uninstall shadcn` and commit the lockfile. Generate future components with `npx shadcn@latest add <name>` on demand; `components.json` keeps working. Do not stop at moving it to `devDependencies`: Vercel installs devDependencies for Next.js builds, so the install scripts would still run. Re-run `npm audit` afterwards; expect it to drop from 15 to roughly 4 lines. Optionally evaluate `ignore-scripts=true` in `.npmrc` (test the build first; `sharp` 0.35 ships prebuilt binaries via optional dependencies and should not need a script).

**Where:** `package.json:37`, `package-lock.json`, `src/app/globals.css:3`, `components.json`, every Vercel build (`npm ci`).

## Remediation Roadmap

_Scope note: only the first three of the 20 confirmed findings reached this step (the list handed to the roadmap writer was cut off mid-way). The actions below cover those three: logins that never expire, password changes with no check, and the shared email budget. Actions for the other 17 need to be added from the sections that follow._

### Immediate (this week)

- Add a captcha (the "prove you're human" box, Cloudflare Turnstile or hCaptcha) to sign-up, sign-in, password reset and "resend code", then switch captcha on in Supabase → Auth → Attack protection so the login service rejects requests without one (code, ~3h; Supabase dashboard, 5 min) — after this, a script with the site's public key can no longer fire off hundreds of reset and sign-in emails, so it can no longer use up the hourly email budget that every new student's sign-up code depends on.
- Rewrite the three Supabase-sent email templates the app never uses (Reset password, Magic link, Change email) so each links with a one-time code to the app's own page (`/auth/update-password?token_hash=…&type=recovery`, which already reads that shape) instead of the old `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}` (Supabase dashboard, ~30 min plus one test email each) — after this, a university email scanner can no longer "use up" a student's reset link before the student clicks it, and a reset link no longer dumps the student on the home page where nothing happens.
- In Supabase → Auth → Sessions, give every login a maximum life ("time-box", e.g. 30 days) and an idle cut-off ("inactivity timeout", e.g. 7 days); both are Pro-plan switches, so upgrade first if the project is still on Free (Supabase dashboard, 5 min) — after this, a stolen or forgotten login stops working on its own within days instead of lasting until the student happens to sign out everywhere.
- Turn on "Secure password change" in Supabase → Auth (the setting that makes an old login prove itself again before it may set a new password); the reset page already has the wording for that step, so walk the reset flow once in a browser afterwards (Supabase dashboard, 5 min; browser check, ~20 min) — after this, someone who picks up a signed-in phone or library computer can no longer set a new password and lock the real owner out.
- Turn on the "password changed" and "email changed" notification emails in Supabase → Auth → Emails (Supabase dashboard, 5 min) — after this, a student whose password is changed by someone else gets an email within a minute, instead of finding out when they are locked out.
- Raise the project-wide "emails sent per hour" limit from 40 to a few hundred; the app already sends through its own Resend account, which is what makes this limit editable (Supabase dashboard, 2 min) — after this, a busy launch evening or a small burst of abuse no longer blocks every new student's confirmation code at once.
- Pick a quiet hour and sign everyone out once (one admin call per user, or the Supabase dashboard), so the 69 leftover logins for 21 people are cleared before the new expiry rules take over (Supabase, ~10 min) — after this, a login someone forgot on a shared computer months ago can no longer be picked up and used.

### Short term (this month)

- Send a Content-Security-Policy from `src/proxy.ts` (a rule the site hands the browser listing exactly which scripts may run, each tagged with a one-time nonce; also `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'self'`), and move the inline scripts in `public/html/*.html` into separate files so the policy can forbid inline code (code, ~1 day including a full browser walk at phone width) — after this, even if an attacker manages to sneak a script onto a Vibe page, the browser refuses to run it, so that script can no longer copy a student's login and send it away.
- Put the three admin accounts behind an authenticator-app code: each admin enrols a code generator (the Supabase side is already switched on), and `requirePlatformAdmin` refuses any login that did not pass that second step (the `aal` claim in the session) (code, ~3h; each admin, 5 min to enrol) — after this, stealing an admin's login is no longer enough to reach the moderation tools; the attacker would also need the admin's phone.
- On the "already signed in, no link" branch of `/auth/update-password`, ask for the current password (or send the person through a fresh reset link) before accepting a new one (code, ~2h) — after this, holding an unlocked phone is no longer enough to change the owner's password; the old one has to be typed too.
- Add a "Sign out everywhere" button in Settings that ends every login for that account (the app's logout already knows how to do this) (code, ~1h) — after this, a student who left Vibe open on a roommate's laptop can shut that door from their own phone in one tap.
- When a password changes, also drop that account's registered push devices and other logins the same way logout does (code, ~1h) — after this, changing your password also silently disconnects any stranger's phone from your notifications.

### Ongoing / process

- Re-run the security sentinel after each hardening pass and before each tester wave, and read the "fixed / still open" line first (process, ~1h per run) — after this, a protection that quietly stops working gets caught by us instead of by a student.
- Keep a one-page checklist of the Supabase Auth settings that matter (session limits, secure password change, captcha, email rate limit, notification emails) and tick it at every milestone (process, 15 min to write, 5 min per check) — after this, a switch flipped back by accident is caught the same week instead of months later.
- Make "no inline scripts" the rule for every new page, including the `/html` desktop shells and anything Otto renders (process, reviewed on each PR) — after this, a new page can no longer quietly reopen the door the browser rule closed.
- Once a month, compare the number of live logins with the number of users (process, 5 min) — after this, a pile-up of forgotten logins like today's 69-for-21 is noticed before it becomes a way in.

### Needs a specialist

- Before the Oct 16 store submission and the campus-wide launch, pay for a live pentest against a staging copy of the site (someone whose job is breaking websites attacks a copy on purpose, with the captcha, session limits and browser rule already in place so they test the finished version) (staging pentest, budget item plus ~1 day of fixes afterwards) — after this, the first person to seriously try to break Vibe is someone we hired, not a student with a grudge.
